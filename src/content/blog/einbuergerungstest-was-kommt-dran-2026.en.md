Perhaps the most important question before you start preparing is: **What actually comes up in the citizenship test?** Many people imagine a huge political exam. In reality, the test is much easier to plan for.

The topics are known, the question catalogue is public and the structure is clear. If you know which areas are tested, you study more calmly and more quickly.

## The short answer: it is about understanding Germany
The citizenship test checks basic knowledge about Germany. It is not about writing long texts or holding complicated political debates.

You have to answer typical questions about:

- democracy and the rule of law
- fundamental rights and duties
- history and responsibility
- society and living together
- elections, parties and state institutions
- your federal state

## Topic 1: Living in a democracy
This area is particularly important. It is about Germany's political system.

Typical questions are about:

- the Bundestag, Bundesrat and federal government
- elections and parties
- separation of powers
- the rule of law
- freedom of expression and fundamental rights

Many mistakes happen because terms sound similar. So always learn institutions in groups: the Bundestag, Bundesrat and federal government belong together.

## Topic 2: History and responsibility
This is about important stages of German history. Questions on the National Socialist past, the Federal Republic, the GDR and reunification are particularly common.

You do not have to know every detail of history by heart. A simple timeline with key points helps:

- National Socialism and the Second World War
- founding of the Federal Republic of Germany
- the GDR and the division of Germany
- building of the Wall and the peaceful revolution
- reunification

Once you understand the order, many individual questions become easier.

## Topic 3: People and society
This area often seems easy because it is close to everyday life. Still, careful study pays off.

It covers, for example:

- equality
- freedom of religion
- family and school
- living together in a pluralistic society
- social responsibility

Watch the wording. Some answers sound "nice" but are not legally correct.

## Topic 4: Federal state questions
In the real test you get 3 questions about your federal state. The preparation catalogue contains 10 questions about the state you live in.

They are often about:

- the state parliament (Landtag)
- the state government
- the Minister President
- the coat of arms or regional features
- the state capital

The classic mistake: practising with the wrong federal state. Set your state right at the start.

## What does not come up?
The citizenship test is not a language essay, an interview or an oral political discussion. You do not have to write long definitions either.

You answer multiple-choice questions. Each question has four answer options, one of which is correct.

That does not mean pure memorisation is always enough, though. If you understand the terms, you recognise correct answers more quickly and avoid mix-ups.

## How many questions should I study?
To prepare, you should know the official question catalogue: **300 general questions** and **10 questions about your federal state**. The BAMF provides the catalogue and sample papers in its **[Online Test Centre](https://www.bamf.de/DE/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/OnlineTestcenter/online-testcenter-node.html)**.

In the real exam you get 33 questions: 30 general ones and 3 about your state.

## The best order to study in
If you are just starting, do not study everything at once. This order works better:

1. get an overview of the topics
2. practise the general questions in small blocks
3. mark difficult terms
4. study the federal state questions separately
5. take a complete exam simulation

This way you always know where you stand.

## Mini study plan for complete beginners
If you are starting today, give yourself 7 days to get going:

- Day 1: understand the test structure and set your federal state
- Day 2: democracy and the state
- Day 3: fundamental rights and duties
- Day 4: history and reunification
- Day 5: society and living together
- Day 6: federal state questions
- Day 7: first exam simulation

After this week you will know which topics are easy and where you need to revise in a targeted way.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Which topics are most important in the citizenship test?
Very important are democracy, the rule of law, fundamental rights, German history, society and the questions about your federal state.

### Do the same questions always come up?
The exam is based on the official question catalogue. Which 33 questions you actually get varies.

### Do I have to learn all federal states?
No. For your exam, the questions about the state where your main residence is registered are relevant.

### Is it enough to learn only the federal state questions?
No. Most questions in the exam are general questions. The state questions are important but only part of the test.

### What is the hardest topic?
Many people mix up political institutions and election terms. The Bundestag, Bundesrat, federal government, first vote and second vote in particular should be second nature.
