If you want to be naturalised in Lower Saxony (Niedersachsen), the way your commitment to the free democratic basic order is handled can vary greatly. **In the City of Hannover** (Landeshauptstadt Hannover), applicants generally receive a **written question catalogue**. **In Hannover Region** (Region Hannover), by contrast, there is no general check of applicants' inner attitude. Salzgitter asks about polygamy and equality, Wolfsburg uses two questionnaires, and in Braunschweig a signature is usually enough.

This is shown by a survey the state government carried out among all nationality authorities in the state, published in May 2025 as [state parliament paper 19/7141](https://www.landtag-niedersachsen.de/drucksachen/drucksachen_19_07500/07001-07500/19-07141.pdf). We have analysed the answers and divided them into three groups.

> **Note:** The information reflects what the offices themselves reported in spring 2025. Practice may have changed since then, for example after the ruling of the Administrative Court of Braunschweig. The division into three models is our own summary. If in doubt, ask your office how the appointment works.

## Key points

- The law does not prescribe **how** the commitment (Bekenntnis) is checked. The offices decide for themselves.
- Three models: **questions for everyone** (written or oral), **conversation or explanation when signing** and **signature only, questioning only where there are indications**.
- The Lower Saxony Ministry of the Interior views routine "knowledge tests" without a reason critically, but considers questions in the course of explaining the commitment to be permissible.
- In 2025, the Administrative Court of Braunschweig ruled that questioning without a reason has no legal basis in the Nationality Act (the Peine district case). The ruling is a first-instance decision.
- Regardless of the model, many offices mention standard enquiries with the Office for the Protection of the Constitution (Verfassungsschutz), the police or the Federal Central Criminal Register (Bundeszentralregister), and a review of the applicant's immigration file (Ausländerakte).

## Model 1: Questions for all applicants

These offices stated that they generally question all applicants or ask comprehension questions:

- **City of Hannover**: Generally a **written question catalogue**, including questions on Germany's historical responsibility. Answers preferably in full sentences; the caseworker assesses them.
- **City of Salzgitter**: Questions when the application is submitted, e.g.: Which parties are there? What does democracy mean? Who is the Federal Chancellor? What do you think of polygamy? What is your position on equality between men and women?
- **City of Wolfsburg**: Two short questionnaires on the commitment and the declaration of loyalty (Loyalitätserklärung). If basic knowledge is missing, a new appointment after 4–6 weeks, and after that a refusal if necessary.
- **Aurich district**: Short questioning: What is our constitution called? What form of state do we have? Two or three basic rights. Reference to the historical responsibility.
- **City of Emden**: Questions on the information sheet, e.g. explaining the separation of powers or the independence of the courts in your own words, two or three basic rights.
- **Friesland district**: Always an explanation and questioning after the appointment; written confirmation.
- **Gifhorn district**: Conversation: summarise the content in your own words; otherwise a repeat appointment.
- **Holzminden district**: Summarise the content in your own words, followed by questions from the caseworker.
- **Cloppenburg district**: Comprehension questions, followed by questions on inner conviction.
- **City of Hildesheim**: Summons, comprehension questions; the record is signed by both sides.
- **Hildesheim district**: Explanation and a few questions on the content; results are recorded in writing.
- **City of Delmenhorst**: Detailed personal conversation about the commitment and the historical responsibility.
- **Nienburg district**: Comprehension questions after signing; if there is uncertainty, an extra appointment with questions on individual passages.

## Model 2: Conversation or explanation when signing

Here the commitment is explained and discussed without working through a fixed question catalogue:

- **City of Celle**: A four-page form is discussed. If there are doubts, standardised or individual question catalogues based on the naturalisation test, the commitment and the information sheet.
- **Emsland district**: Explanation of the 2024 changes: war of aggression, antisemitism, racism and Germany's role towards Judaism; question whether this reflects your own conviction.
- **City of Göttingen**: Asks whether the information sheet has been understood; further questions only if there are doubts.
- **Helmstedt district**: Short conversation about the meaning of the basic order; more detailed in individual cases.
- **Diepholz district**: Oral information; occasional follow-up questions if there are doubts, no general questioning.
- **Wittmund district**: Free conversation about the content, no set guideline; a new appointment if necessary.
- **Northeim district**: Conversation about the documents, no standardised questionnaire.
- **Oldenburg district**: Written and oral explanation; no knowledge test with a questionnaire.
- **Heidekreis district**: Asks whether the content has been understood; summons only if there are concerns.
- **Verden district**: Explanation; depending on indications, more or fewer questions on comprehension and attitudes.
- **Rotenburg district**: Explanation based on the information leaflet; questions in individual cases, expressly no questions on attitudes.
- **Schaumburg district**: Explanation and clarification of open questions.
- **Uelzen district**: The commitment is recited aloud together at the naturalisation ceremony.

## Model 3: Signature, questioning only where there are indications

These offices stated that they generally rely on the signed commitment and only ask further questions if there are concrete doubts, for example after information from the immigration file, the police or the Office for the Protection of the Constitution:

- **Hannover Region** (no general check of inner attitude; an individually prepared conversation if there are grounds for suspicion)
- **City of Braunschweig**, **City of Lüneburg**, **City of Osnabrück**, **Osnabrück district**, **City of Oldenburg**, **City of Wilhelmshaven**
- **Peine district** (according to its own statement, an open-ended conversation with its own question catalogue only where there are indications)
- **City of Hameln** (until recently questioning in every case, switch to questioning only where there is a reason announced), **Hameln-Pyrmont district**
- **Celle district**, **Göttingen district**, **Harburg district**, **Lüchow-Dannenberg district**, **Stade district**, **Vechta district**, **Ammerland district**, **Goslar district**, **Osterholz district**, **Leer district**, **Wesermarsch district**
- **Cuxhaven district** (simple questions in individual cases, for example if school or the test was a long time ago), **City of Cuxhaven**

The printed paper contained no information for Grafschaft Bentheim district, the City of Lingen and Wolfenbüttel district.

## Hannover: city or region?

For many readers this is the most important question, because the procedures differ significantly:

- **If you live in the city of Hannover**, the City of Hannover (Landeshauptstadt) is usually responsible. According to its information to the state parliament, a **written question catalogue** is standard there. The questions should preferably be answered in full sentences.
- **If you live in another town or municipality in Hannover Region**, **Hannover Region** is usually responsible. According to its information, there is no general check there, only a conversation if there are concrete facts or grounds for suspicion.

Check which office is responsible for where you live on your municipality's website before you apply.

## Why does practice vary so much?

Since the 2024 reform, the commitment must be based on genuine conviction. But the law does not say how offices should establish this. The Lower Saxony Ministry of the Interior takes a two-step approach:

1. When the commitment is explained, applicants may be asked whether they understand and agree with its content.
2. The ministry considers further investigations unnecessary and not intended by the legislature if there are no doubts about loyalty to the constitution and no concrete indications that someone has not even begun to understand the content.

In February 2025, the **Administrative Court of Braunschweig** went even further: in its view, questioning without a reason has no legal basis at all, and basic knowledge is proven by the naturalisation test. We explain the case in the article **[Refused after 23 questions: the Peine ruling](/en/blog/einbuergerung-abgelehnt-23-fragen-peine-urteil/)**.

## How to prepare, whichever model applies

1. **Read the information sheet on the free democratic basic order** that many offices send out with the application documents. Translations are available in many languages.
2. **Understand the declaration of loyalty point by point.** We explain it in the article **[The declaration of loyalty for German citizenship](/en/blog/loyalitaetserklaerung-einbuergerung/)**.
3. **Practise explaining the core ideas in your own words:** democracy, the rule of law, separation of powers, basic rights, equality, religious freedom, the ban on wars of aggression, responsibility for Jewish life.
4. **Prepare short answers in full sentences.** You will find sample questions and model answers in the guide **[German citizenship interview questions](/en/blog/einbuergerung-gespraech-fragen-bekenntnis/)**.
5. **Revise the content of the naturalisation test**, even if you have already passed it. Many of the offices' questions are similar to the test questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### May I ask whether my appointment includes questioning?

Yes. When making the appointment, ask whether a conversation or a questionnaire on the commitment is planned and whether you will receive an information sheet in advance.

### What if I do not answer the questions well at the first appointment?

Several offices offer a second appointment in this case, sometimes after a few weeks. Use the time to prepare. If your application is refused because of your answers, get advice.

### Does this only apply to Lower Saxony?

The printed paper only covers Lower Saxony. There are also offices in other federal states that hold conversations or ask questions. Your naturalisation office can tell you how it works in your city.
