Yes, it happens: when you hand in your citizenship application, or at a later appointment, some German naturalisation offices (Einbürgerungsbehörde) ask **additional questions about your commitment to the free democratic basic order**. This can be a short conversation or a **questionnaire you answer with pen and paper in full sentences**. Typical topics are equality between men and women, religious freedom, the ban on wars of aggression (for example Russia's war against Ukraine) and Germany's responsibility to protect Jewish life.

This questionnaire is **not the official naturalisation test** (Einbürgerungstest / Leben in Deutschland test). There is no national question catalogue for it and no pass mark. Each office decides whether and how to ask. In Lower Saxony alone, practice ranges from a simple signature to a written questionnaire for every applicant.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not legal advice. Practice varies and changes, and the legality of questioning applicants without a specific reason has not been settled by Germany's highest administrative court. If you face a refusal, contact a migration advice service or a lawyer specialising in migration law.

## Key points

- Under [Section 10(1) sentence 1 nos. 1 and 1a of the Nationality Act (StAG)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html), you must commit to the free democratic basic order and to **Germany's special historical responsibility** for the Nazi regime. This includes protecting Jewish life, peaceful coexistence between nations and the ban on wars of aggression.
- Since the 2024 reform, this commitment must reflect your **genuine conviction**. Lip service is not enough.
- If factual indications suggest that your commitment is untrue, naturalisation is excluded under [Section 11 sentence 1 no. 1a StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__11.html).
- The law does not say how offices must check this. Some only ask for a signature, others hold a conversation or hand out a questionnaire.
- According to the Lower Saxony state government, applicants at the **City of Hannover generally receive a written question catalogue**, which should be answered in full sentences and includes questions on Germany's historical responsibility.
- In 2025, the Administrative Court of Braunschweig ruled that **questioning every applicant without a specific reason** has no legal basis in the Nationality Act. This is a first-instance ruling; a decision by the highest court is still pending.
- Best preparation: understand the text of the declaration and be able to explain its core ideas in your own simple sentences, in German.

## Where do these questions come from?

During the procedure you sign a **declaration of loyalty (Loyalitätserklärung)** that includes these commitments. In the [Hesse template (06/2024)](https://innen.hessen.de/sites/innen.hessen.de/files/2024-06/anlage_04-loyalitaetserklaerung_und_bekenntnisse.pdf), you recognise, among other things:

- the right of the people to elect parliament in free, equal and secret elections,
- that government, administration and courts are bound by law,
- the right to form a parliamentary opposition and to vote a government out,
- the independence of the courts and the exclusion of any rule by force or arbitrary rule,
- the human rights set out in the Basic Law (Grundgesetz),
- that antisemitic, racist or other acts motivated by contempt for human beings are incompatible with human dignity.

You also commit to **Germany's special historical responsibility for the Nazi regime and its consequences, in particular for the protection of Jewish life**, and to **peaceful coexistence between nations and the ban on waging a war of aggression**.

Offices derive their questions from exactly these points. That is why the same topics keep coming up:

| Topic in the interview | Basis in the declaration and the law |
| --- | --- |
| Equality of men and women | Basic rights, Article 3(2) Basic Law; exclusion ground in Section 11 sentence 1 no. 3 StAG |
| Religious freedom | Basic rights, Article 4 Basic Law; religiously neutral state |
| Russia–Ukraine war | Peaceful coexistence, ban on wars of aggression (Article 26 Basic Law, Section 10(1) no. 1a StAG) |
| Judaism, Holocaust, Israel | Historical responsibility, protection of Jewish life, rejection of antisemitism |
| Democracy, separation of powers, elections | Core elements of the free democratic basic order |

## How do offices check? The example of Lower Saxony

In spring 2025, the Lower Saxony government asked every naturalisation office in the state how it checks the declaration. The answers were published in [state parliament paper 19/7141](https://www.landtag-niedersachsen.de/drucksachen/drucksachen_19_07500/07001-07500/19-07141.pdf) in May 2025:

- **City of Hannover:** The declaration is part of the application form. Applicants **generally receive a question catalogue**, including questions on Germany's historical responsibility. Answers should be written in full sentences and are assessed by the caseworker.
- **Hannover Region** (the surrounding towns): No general check of applicants' inner attitude. A prepared interview only takes place if concrete facts or suspicions arise.
- **City of Salzgitter:** Questions at submission, for example what democracy means, what you think of polygamy and how you view equality between men and women.
- **Aurich district and City of Emden:** Short questions such as "What is our constitution called?", "What form of state do we have?" or "Name two or three basic rights". In Emden, applicants may be asked to explain the separation of powers in their own words.
- **Emsland district:** An explanation of the 2024 changes, especially on wars of aggression, antisemitism, racism and Germany's role towards Jewish people, followed by the question whether this reflects your conviction.
- **City of Wolfsburg:** Two short questionnaires; if basic knowledge is missing, a second appointment four to six weeks later.
- **Many other offices** (for example City of Braunschweig, City of Lüneburg, Oldenburg district): Signature only; questions only if there are concrete doubts.

Similar interviews also take place outside Lower Saxony. There are no uniform rules and no published national question list. You can simply ask your office in advance how the appointment works. A full list of Lower Saxony offices: **[Naturalisation in Lower Saxony](/en/blog/einbuergerung-niedersachsen-bekenntnis-befragung/)**.

## Is this kind of questioning allowed?

There are two sides.

**For questions:** Since 2024, the declaration is expressly a substantive requirement and must be based on genuine conviction. The Lower Saxony government considers it permissible to ask, when explaining the declaration, whether you understand and agree with its content.

**Against questions without a reason:** On 20 February 2025, the [Administrative Court of Braunschweig](https://verwaltungsgericht-braunschweig.niedersachsen.de/startseite/aktuelles/pressemitteilungen/verwaltungsgericht-beanstandet-einburgerungspraxis-im-landkreis-peine-239719.html) (case 4 A 114/24) overturned the refusal of a Lebanese applicant by Peine district. He had, among other things, B1 German and a passed Leben in Deutschland test. The office had asked him 23 questions, for example about democracy, the rule of law, the news, mosque visits and whether he watches German TV, and refused him because of his answers. The court held:

- Without concrete indications that cast doubt on an applicant's loyalty to the constitution, there is **no legal basis for questioning without a reason**.
- Basic knowledge of the state and society is proven by the **passed naturalisation test**. The office may not add its own knowledge test.
- Many questions had no objectively verifiable correct answer or no clear link to the basic order.

The office has to decide again. The court allowed an appeal and a leapfrog appeal to the Federal Administrative Court. We found no decision by the Federal Administrative Court in this case at the time of writing. More detail: **[Refused after 23 questions: the Peine ruling](/en/blog/einbuergerung-abgelehnt-23-fragen-peine-urteil/)**.

**In practice:** Do not simply refuse the questionnaire. A refusal can be seen as a reason for doubt and will delay your case. Answer calmly and honestly. If your application is about to be refused because of your answers, get advice. The Braunschweig ruling is then an important argument.

## The four topics: what the office wants to know

It is usually **not about detailed knowledge**, but about whether you understand the basic idea and honestly stand behind it. A good answer has three parts: **the rule** (what applies in Germany), **an example** from everyday life and **your own position**.

The questions below are practice examples based on the text of the declaration and on reports about interviews. They are not an official question catalogue of any office. The sample answers are in German, because you will usually have to answer in German. Use them to learn phrases, then write your own sentences.

### 1. Equality of men and women

Article 3(2) of the Basic Law says: "Männer und Frauen sind gleichberechtigt" (Men and women have equal rights). The state must promote actual equality. Anyone whose conduct shows disregard for this equality, or who is married to more than one spouse at the same time, cannot be naturalised under [Section 11 sentence 1 no. 3 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__11.html).

Possible questions:

- What does equality between men and women mean to you?
- May a woman in Germany work, open a bank account or get divorced without her husband's permission?
- What do you think of polygamy?

Sample answer:

> In Deutschland haben Frauen und Männer die gleichen Rechte. Eine Frau entscheidet selbst, ob sie arbeitet, welchen Beruf sie hat, wen sie heiratet und ob sie sich scheiden lässt. In meiner Familie treffen meine Frau und ich wichtige Entscheidungen gemeinsam. Meine Tochter soll dieselben Chancen haben wie mein Sohn. Eine Ehe mit mehreren Partnern ist in Deutschland nicht erlaubt, und das finde ich richtig.

*In English:* Women and men have equal rights in Germany. A woman decides for herself whether she works, which job she does, whom she marries and whether she gets divorced. In my family, my wife and I make important decisions together. My daughter should have the same opportunities as my son. Marriage to several partners is not allowed in Germany, and I think that is right.

### 2. Religious freedom

Article 4 of the Basic Law protects freedom of faith and conscience. Everyone may have a religion, change it or have none. The state is religiously neutral, and its laws apply to everyone regardless of religious rules.

Possible questions:

- What does religious freedom mean?
- What applies if a religious rule contradicts a German law?
- May people in Germany change their religion or criticise religion?

Sample answer:

> Religionsfreiheit bedeutet, dass jeder Mensch selbst entscheidet, ob und woran er glaubt. Man darf seine Religion ausüben, wechseln oder ganz ohne Religion leben. Niemand darf deshalb benachteiligt oder bedroht werden. Der Staat bevorzugt keine Religion. Wenn eine religiöse Regel einem Gesetz widerspricht, gilt das Gesetz. Ich respektiere auch Menschen, die anders glauben als ich oder meine Religion kritisieren.

*In English:* Religious freedom means that everyone decides for themselves whether and what they believe. You may practise your religion, change it or live without religion. Nobody may be disadvantaged or threatened for that. The state does not favour any religion. If a religious rule contradicts a law, the law applies. I also respect people who believe differently or criticise my religion.

### 3. Russia's war against Ukraine: the ban on wars of aggression

Since 2024, applicants expressly commit to **peaceful coexistence between nations and the ban on waging a war of aggression**. Article 26 of the Basic Law declares acts preparing a war of aggression unconstitutional. A question about the war in Ukraine is usually meant to show whether you understand and can apply this principle.

Possible questions:

- How do you assess Russia's war against Ukraine?
- What does "ban on wars of aggression" mean?
- May a state change another state's borders by force?

Sample answer:

> Russland hat die Ukraine im Februar 2022 angegriffen. Das ist ein Angriffskrieg, weil ein Staat einen anderen Staat überfallen hat, um mit Gewalt Gebiet und Kontrolle zu gewinnen. Nach dem Grundgesetz und dem Völkerrecht ist ein Angriffskrieg verboten. Konflikte zwischen Staaten müssen friedlich gelöst werden. Ich bin dafür, dass Völker friedlich zusammenleben und Grenzen nicht mit Gewalt verändert werden.

*In English:* Russia attacked Ukraine in February 2022. This is a war of aggression, because one state invaded another to gain territory and control by force. Under the Basic Law and international law, a war of aggression is prohibited. Conflicts between states must be resolved peacefully. I support peaceful coexistence and oppose changing borders by force.

You do not need to write a political analysis. What matters is that you distinguish attack from defence and clearly support the ban on wars of aggression.

### 4. Jewish life, the Holocaust and antisemitism

Germany bears a **special responsibility** for the crimes of National Socialism. Between 1933 and 1945, around six million European Jews were murdered (Holocaust, Shoah). This gives rise to the duty to protect Jewish life in Germany and to oppose antisemitism. Since 2024, the official naturalisation test also includes questions on Jewish life, antisemitism and Israel, for example [question 288](/en/frage/288/) on Germany's special responsibility for Israel.

Whether offices may demand an explicit commitment to **Israel's right to exist** is disputed. The law does not mention Israel. Some federal states and courts, however, treat recognition of Israel's right to exist as part of the historical responsibility. Expect to be asked about it. Details by federal state: **[Israel's right to exist and German citizenship](/en/blog/einbuergerung-existenzrecht-israel/)**.

Possible questions:

- Why does Germany have a special responsibility towards Jewish people?
- What is antisemitism? Give an example.
- What is your position on Israel's right to exist?
- How would you react if someone around you insulted Jewish people?

Sample answer:

> Im Nationalsozialismus hat Deutschland etwa sechs Millionen Jüdinnen und Juden ermordet. Deshalb hat Deutschland eine besondere Verantwortung: Jüdisches Leben in Deutschland muss geschützt werden, und Antisemitismus darf keinen Platz haben. Antisemitismus bedeutet Hass oder Vorurteile gegen Jüdinnen und Juden, zum Beispiel Beleidigungen, Verschwörungsmythen oder Angriffe auf Synagogen. Jüdinnen und Juden in Deutschland sind nicht für die Politik eines anderen Staates verantwortlich. Ich erkenne das Existenzrecht Israels an. Wenn jemand Juden beleidigt, widerspreche ich und hole im Notfall Hilfe.

*In English:* Under National Socialism, Germany murdered about six million Jews. That is why Germany has a special responsibility: Jewish life in Germany must be protected, and antisemitism has no place. Antisemitism means hatred of or prejudice against Jewish people, for example insults, conspiracy myths or attacks on synagogues. Jewish people in Germany are not responsible for the policies of another state. I recognise Israel's right to exist. If someone insults Jewish people, I speak up and get help in an emergency.

Criticising a government's policies is allowed in Germany, including the Israeli government's. It becomes antisemitic when Jewish people are blamed collectively for those policies or when Israel's right to exist is denied.

## Other common interview questions

Many offices also ask general questions about the declaration. Prepare short answers to:

- **Why do you want to become German?** Give concrete reasons, such as the right to vote, belonging or your children's future.
- **What does democracy mean?** The people vote in free and secret elections, there are several parties and an opposition, and the government can be voted out.
- **What is the rule of law (Rechtsstaat)?** The state itself must obey the law; independent courts control it.
- **What is the separation of powers?** Legislature, executive (government and administration) and judiciary (courts) are separate and check each other.
- **Name some basic rights.** For example human dignity (Art. 1), equality (Art. 3), religious freedom (Art. 4), freedom of expression (Art. 5).
- **What is the constitution called and what form of state is Germany?** The Basic Law (Grundgesetz); a parliamentary democracy and federal state with 16 states.

## How to answer a written questionnaire

1. **Read all questions first** and plan your time. Ask at the start how much time you have.
2. **Write full sentences**; three to five per question are enough. Bullet points often look too brief.
3. **Rule, example, position:** What applies in Germany? What does it look like in everyday life? What do you think?
4. **Simple German is fine.** Clear content matters more than perfect spelling.
5. **Avoid qualifiers.** Phrases like "Yes, but in my culture…" or "It depends…" can easily read as a reservation against the declaration.
6. **Be honest.** Memorised sentences you cannot explain will not help if the caseworker asks follow-up questions.
7. **If you do not understand a question**, ask the caseworker what is meant.

## Your rights in the procedure

- **Preparation:** Ask for the information sheet on the free democratic basic order. The Federal Ministry of the Interior has provided translations in more than 30 languages to the federal states. Read the German version too.
- **Copy:** Ask for a copy of your questionnaire or the interview record. If you are asked to sign a record, read it carefully and have mistakes corrected.
- **Access to your file:** During the procedure you can generally request access to your file where this is necessary to protect your legal interests.
- **Second appointment:** Many offices offer a new appointment instead of refusing straight away.
- **Refusal:** Check the legal remedy information (Rechtsbehelfsbelehrung) in the decision and the deadline. Get advice early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does everyone have to fill in this questionnaire?

No. It depends on the office. Many offices only ask you to sign the declaration, others question every applicant. According to the state government, a question catalogue is standard at the City of Hannover, but not at Hannover Region.

### Is this a second naturalisation test?

No. The official Leben in Deutschland test with 33 questions is the legal proof of your knowledge. The interview is about whether you understand what you are committing to. Preparing for the test still helps, because many topics overlap. Practise the **[naturalisation test questions](/en/fragen/)**, especially on basic rights and history.

### Can I be refused because of this?

Yes, if the office concludes from your answers, based on factual indications, that your declaration does not reflect your conviction, for example because you reject equality between men and women or justify antisemitism. The Braunschweig court, however, held that a refusal solely for lack of knowledge, despite a passed naturalisation test, was unlawful.

### Can I answer in my native language?

You generally need German at B1 level for naturalisation, so expect to answer in German. Simple sentences are perfectly fine.

### When does the questioning take place?

It varies: when you submit your application, at an appointment to hand in further documents, or shortly before you receive your naturalisation certificate. For the overall requirements, see **[German citizenship requirements](/en/blog/german-citizenship-requirements/)**.
