The citizenship test takes **60 minutes**. In this time you answer 33 multiple-choice questions. For many participants the time is plenty, but you should still practise the process beforehand.

The reason: the clock is usually not the problem; nerves, careless reading or uncertainty about the answer sheet are.

## 60 minutes for 33 questions
In the exam you get 33 questions. You have 60 minutes. Mathematically, this means you have just under two minutes per question on average.

For multiple-choice questions that is generous. Many candidates finish earlier. Still, you should not rush.

A good rule:

- first round: answer all the questions you are sure about
- second round: check the difficult questions
- last few minutes: check the answer sheet

This way you use the time without getting stuck on a single question.

## How fast should I be per question?
You do not have to answer every question equally quickly. Some questions you recognise immediately, others need more thought.

A practical rhythm:

- easy questions: 20 to 40 seconds
- medium questions: 1 to 2 minutes
- difficult questions: mark them and check later

If you are still unsure about a question after two minutes, move on. One blocked question must not throw your whole exam off course.

## Why many people still make mistakes
The time is usually enough. Even so, mistakes happen because participants go too fast.

Typical time-related mistakes are:

- reading only half the question
- overlooking "not"
- not comparing all the answer options
- ticking the first familiar answer straight away
- not checking at the end

In the citizenship test in particular, small words matter. "May", "must", "only", "always" or "not" can change the correct answer.

## Should I stay for the whole hour?
If you finish early, use the remaining time. Calmly go through your answers once more.

Check in particular:

- Have I answered every question?
- Have I really marked only one answer per question?
- Have I re-read the difficult questions?
- Have I recognised the federal state questions correctly?
- Is my answer sheet filled in neatly?

There is no bonus for handing in especially early. A calm check is often worth more than finishing quickly.

## How do I practise the 60 minutes at home?
Many people only practise single questions. That is good for knowledge but not for exam confidence. Also do complete simulations.

Here is how:

1. select 33 questions
2. set a timer for 60 minutes
3. work through them without a break
4. check the result
5. update your mistake list

Important: do not interrupt the simulation after a wrong answer. In the real test you do not find out the result immediately either.

## What do I do if I read slowly?
If German is not your strongest language, allow more reading time. That is normal.

These strategies help:

- learn common terms beforehand
- note short definitions
- read the answer options silently word by word
- mark signal words
- do not start new topics on the last evening

The more familiar the terms are, the less time you lose reading.

## Official basis
The BAMF states **33 questions** and **60 minutes** for the citizenship test. The official **[sample questionnaire](https://www.bamf.de/SharedDocs/Anlagen/DE/Integration/Einbuergerung/musterbogen_einbuergerungstest.html?nn=284310)** also shows how the questions are laid out on the test paper.

## Quick summary
- The citizenship test takes 60 minutes.
- You answer 33 multiple-choice questions.
- The time is usually enough if you work calmly and in a structured way.
- Use any remaining time to check.
- Practise several complete tests with a timer before your appointment.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Do I have to stay exactly 60 minutes?
The test centre explains the procedure on site. If you finish early, you should still use the time to check.

### Is 60 minutes enough time?
For most people, yes. Difficulties tend to come from nerves or careless reading.

### How many minutes do I have per question?
On average just under two minutes per question. Easy questions need less, difficult ones more.

### May I skip questions and come back later?
In practice you can leave difficult questions open at first and check them later. But make sure you do not forget any question at the end.

### Should I practise with a timer at home?
Yes. Complete 60-minute simulations help you train the real process.
