If you want to naturalise in Berlin, you usually have to pass the **citizenship test** (Einbürgerungstest). In Berlin this is organised in a particularly straightforward way: the test is offered at the districts' **adult education centres (Volkshochschulen, VHS)**, and you can choose any Berlin VHS, regardless of which district you live in.

## Where can I take the citizenship test in Berlin?
According to the State of Berlin's service portal, you can register for the test at **any Berlin adult education centre**. Where you live within Berlin does not matter. Besides the VHS there are other test centres approved by the BAMF. If there is no suitable date at one VHS, it is worth comparing several locations.

## How registration works
1. **Arrange a registration appointment** – you register at the VHS, usually by appointment.
2. **Fill in the registration form** (Teilnehmermeldebogen) – you get it during the consultation at the VHS and fill it in there.
3. **Pay the fee** – the test costs **25 euros**.
4. **Keep the booking confirmation** – you must bring it on exam day.

Important: **The registration day is not the exam day.** You will be told the available exam dates when you register. You need a valid identity document to register.

## What to expect on exam day
- **33 multiple-choice questions**: 30 general questions and **3 questions about Berlin**
- **60 minutes**
- You pass with **at least 17 correct answers**
- Bring: a valid passport or ID card and the booking confirmation

The three state questions come from the **10 Berlin questions** in the official catalogue, for example about Berlin's coat of arms, its districts or the Governing Mayor. You can practise all ten here: [Berlin questions for the citizenship test](/en/berlin-fragen/).

## After the test: result and citizenship application
The test is marked centrally by the BAMF; the certificate arrives a few weeks later. Read more in our article [Citizenship test results: waiting time and certificate](/en/blog/leben-in-deutschland-test-results/).

In Berlin, the **State Office for Immigration (Landesamt für Einwanderung, LEA)** is responsible for the citizenship application itself. You submit your pass certificate there together with the other documents. Our [checklist for the citizenship application](/en/blog/einbuergerung-antrag-checkliste-2026/) shows which documents you need.

## Who does not have to take the test
Not everyone has to take the test. Exempt are, among others, minors under 16 and people who cannot take it because of illness, disability or age – this must be proven with a medical certificate. If you have a German school-leaving certificate, you may be able to prove your knowledge in another way. The LEA decides whether you need the test. More: [Exemption from the citizenship test](/en/blog/einbuergerungstest-befreiung-krankheit-behinderung/).

## Tips for Berlin
- **Register early:** demand is high in Berlin. Plan the test a few months before your application.
- **Compare several VHS:** since any Berlin VHS is possible, you often find a date more quickly.
- **Practise in a targeted way:** with the [exam simulation](/en/exam/) you train the real process with 33 questions.
