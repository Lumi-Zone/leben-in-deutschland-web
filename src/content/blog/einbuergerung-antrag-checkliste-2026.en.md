**You apply for German citizenship at the naturalisation authority (Einbürgerungsbehörde) of the place where you live. You get the application form from that authority, usually as a PDF on its website or through an online portal.** In practice there is no single nationwide form that every authority accepts. Each authority decides which forms, declarations and copies it wants and whether you apply online, by post or in person.

This checklist is a guide, not legal advice. Your authority's own list is what counts.

## Where do I apply?

The responsible office is the **naturalisation authority (Staatsangehörigkeitsbehörde) for your main place of residence**. Depending on the federal state, this is the city administration, the district office (Landratsamt) or a state authority. Search your city's website for "Einbürgerung" or "Staatsangehörigkeit".

If you live abroad, contact the Federal Office of Administration (Bundesverwaltungsamt).

## Application form: PDF, online or by post?

That depends on your authority:

- **Online:** some states and cities offer online applications. According to the Bavarian Ministry of the Interior, in Bavaria you can apply online or on paper at the naturalisation authority.
- **By post:** other authorities only accept written applications. The City of Stuttgart, for example, only accepts applications by post and asks you not to use folders or plastic sleeves.
- **Appointment:** some authorities first give you an advice appointment and hand out the forms there.

Only download forms from your own authority's official website. Forms from other cities are often not accepted.

## Forms that usually come with the application

Besides the application form itself, many authorities ask for further declarations, for example:

- **Declaration of commitment and loyalty:** commitment to the free democratic basic order and to Germany's special historical responsibility; from age 16 ([more on the loyalty declaration](/en/blog/loyalitaetserklaerung-einbuergerung/))
- declaration on criminal offences
- notice on the security check
- data protection consent
- confirmation of employment from your employer

## Checklist: documents required for German citizenship

Typical documents, as listed for example by the City of Stuttgart:

**Identity and family**

- valid passport with residence permit (no passport? See [German citizenship without a passport](/en/blog/einbuergerung-ohne-pass/))
- one biometric passport photo
- birth certificate
- marriage certificate or extract from the family register, if married
- children's birth certificates; for minor children from divorced marriages, proof of custody
- foreign documents with a translation by a translator recognised in Germany

**Language and knowledge**

- B1 certificate or a German school-leaving qualification
- certificate of the passed citizenship test (from age 16) or a German school-leaving qualification

**Income and work**

- your last three payslips
- proof of the start date of your current employment
- your spouse's proof of income
- notices of any public benefits you receive

**School and training**

- school-leaving certificates; for pupils, their current school reports

The authority may ask for further evidence.

## Tips for filling in the forms

- **Write names consistently:** exactly as in your passport, the same in every form.
- **Avoid gaps:** list all places of residence, periods of stay and jobs completely.
- **Submit only what is requested:** extra documents nobody asked for do not speed up the check.
- **Copies and originals:** ask whether copies are enough and when you need to show originals.
- **Get help:** the naturalisation authority and migration counselling services help with questions about the form.

## How much does it cost?

The fee is **€255 per adult** and **€51 per minor child without their own income who is naturalised with a parent** ([Section 38 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__38.html)). Your authority tells you when and how to pay.

## What happens next?

The authority checks your details, makes enquiries and contacts you if anything is missing. Processing often takes several months. All the conditions you need to meet are explained in [German citizenship requirements](/en/blog/german-citizenship-requirements/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I apply before I have passed the citizenship test?

Many authorities accept the application and let you submit proof later. You will only be naturalised once the proof is there, unless you are exempt. Passing the test first saves time: [how to register for the test](/en/blog/anmeldung-pruefung-schritte/).

### Is there a separate application for dual citizenship?

No. You file the normal naturalisation application; since June 2024 you no longer have to give up your previous citizenship.

### I have submitted my application. Can I still study for the test?

Yes. Practise [all 300 questions with answers](/en/fragen/), the questions for your [federal state](/en/fragen/#bundeslaender) and the [33-question mock exam](/en/exam/). The [Leben in Deutschland app](/en/app/) shows your progress and lets you repeat the questions you got wrong.
