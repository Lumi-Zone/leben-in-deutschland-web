**You do not get your score on the day of the test. The paper is marked centrally by BAMF, and the official certificate usually arrives several weeks later.** For the naturalization test (Einbürgerungstest), BAMF normally sends the certificate by post to your home address. For the Leben in Deutschland (LiD) test taken at the end of an integration course, certificates usually go to the test centre, which hands them out.

## Key facts

- There is no guaranteed number of days. Plan for **several weeks**.
- You cannot check your real result online.
- Naturalization test: certificate by post to the address on your test form.
- LiD test in an integration course: certificate usually collected from the test centre or course provider.
- For naturalization you need **at least 17 of 33** correct answers.
- If the result is late, check your address first, then contact your test centre.

## Why does it take several weeks?

The test is taken on paper. After your appointment, the test centre sends the completed papers to the responsible BAMF regional office. BAMF evaluates them in a standard process and creates the certificate.

1. You hand in your answer sheet.
2. The test centre checks and sends the papers.
3. BAMF marks the answers.
4. The certificate is created.
5. You receive it by post or via the test centre, depending on the test type.

The supervisors are not allowed to tell you your score on the day. Only the official certificate counts.

If your naturalization authority has set you a deadline, tell it early that you have taken the test and are waiting for the result.

## Can I check my result online?

No. The [BAMF Online Test Centre](https://oet.bamf.de/ords/oetut/f?p=514:1:0) is for practice only: it shows your score for practice questionnaires, not your exam. Be careful with websites that claim to look up your personal result with your name and date of birth.

## What does the certificate say?

The certificate shows your total score and the outcome. For the naturalization test:

- **17 to 33 correct:** passed, valid as proof for naturalization
- **0 to 16 correct:** taken, but not enough for naturalization

The LiD test at the end of the orientation course uses the same questions. There, 15 correct answers are enough to pass the course, but for citizenship you still need 17. The difference is explained in [Leben in Deutschland vs. naturalization test](/en/blog/unterschied-lid-test-einbuergerungstest/) and [how many correct answers you need](/en/blog/wie-viele-richtige-antworten-brauche-ich-einbuergerungstest/).

## My result has not arrived: what to do

1. **Check your address.** Was it complete on the test form, including your name on the letterbox or "c/o"?
2. **Moved house?** Tell the test centre as early as possible. A mail forwarding order helps but does not replace updating your address.
3. **Contact the test centre.** Have your full name, date and place of birth, test date and location, and your current address ready. The centre cannot tell you your score in advance, but it can tell you the status or the right contact.
4. **Ask BAMF.** For further questions, use the official [BAMF citizens' service](https://www.bamf.de/DE/Service/ServiceCenter/Buergerservice/buergerservice-node.html). Only send personal documents through the secure channels they name.

## Lost certificate: can I get a new one?

Yes. According to the BAMF examination regulations, the regional office can issue a new copy if you return the original for exchange or send a written declaration that it was lost. Contact your former test centre first and ask which BAMF regional office is responsible.

## Did not pass?

You can register for the test again. Each attempt requires a new registration and fee. Use the waiting time well: [practise all 300 questions in English and German](/en/fragen/), focus on the [10 questions for your federal state](/en/fragen/#bundeslaender), and take [33-question practice exams](/en/exam/) until you pass with a safe margin. The [Leben in Deutschland app](/en/app/) shows which questions you got wrong so you can repeat exactly those.
