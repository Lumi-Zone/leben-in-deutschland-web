The **declaration of loyalty** (Loyalitätserklärung) is a form you sign during the naturalisation procedure. In it, you commit to the **free democratic basic order**, to **Germany's special historical responsibility** for the crimes of National Socialism, to the **protection of Jewish life** and to the **ban on wars of aggression**. You also declare that you do not pursue or support any extremist activities.

Your signature is not a formality: since 2024, the commitment must reflect your **genuine conviction**. False statements can lead to refusal, withdrawal of your naturalisation and even a criminal penalty. So read the text carefully. Here we explain it point by point.

> **Note:** The forms are structured differently depending on the federal state. We follow the template of the state of Hesse (version 06/2024), whose content follows the law. The form of your naturalisation office (Einbürgerungsbehörde) is what counts. This article is not legal advice.

## Key points

- The declaration of loyalty implements [Section 10(1) sentence 1 nos. 1 and 1a StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html).
- You choose one of three options: no extremist activities, having turned away from earlier activities, or doubts about whether an activity was extremist.
- You commit to eight basic principles, to the historical responsibility and to peaceful coexistence between nations.
- With your signature, you confirm that you were able to ask questions and have understood the content.
- When you receive your certificate, there is also a short **solemn declaration** (feierliches Bekenntnis) under [Section 16 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__16.html).
- Children under 16 generally do not have to make the commitment themselves.

## Part 1: Which option applies to you?

The form begins with a choice. You tick the statement that applies to you:

1. **I do not pursue or support any extremist activities and have not done so in the past.** This is the standard case.
2. **I pursued or supported such activities in the past but have turned away from them.** You then describe the activities, the period and how you can credibly show that you have turned away, for example with evidence.
3. **I am not sure whether an activity was extremist.** You describe the activity and let the office check it.

Options 2 and 3 are honest ways of disclosing uncertainties. If you tick option 1 instead and conceal something, you risk much more. Offices generally make enquiries with the security authorities, and your information may be passed on to the Office for the Protection of the Constitution (Verfassungsschutz).

**What counts as an extremist activity?** This means activities directed against the free democratic basic order or against the existence or security of the Federation or a federal state, activities aimed at unlawfully impairing constitutional bodies, and violence or preparations for violence that endanger Germany's foreign interests. This can concern, for example, membership in or support for banned or extremist organisations. Many offices hand out an information leaflet on this.

## Part 2: The commitment to the free democratic basic order

In particular, you recognise:

| Point in the form | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| a) The people exercise state authority through elections and votes and elect parliament in general, direct, free, equal and secret elections. | **Democracy:** Citizens decide who governs. Every vote counts equally, and nobody may be forced to vote a certain way. |
| b) The legislature is bound by the constitution; the government, administration and courts are bound by law and justice. | **Rule of law:** The state, too, must follow the rules. Nobody is above the law. |
| c) The right to form and exercise a parliamentary opposition. | **Opposition:** Parties that are not in government may criticise and scrutinise the government. |
| d) The government can be replaced and is accountable to parliament. | **Change of power:** A government can be voted out and must answer to parliament. |
| e) The independence of the courts. | **Separation of powers:** Judges decide free from instructions by the government. |
| f) The exclusion of any rule by force or arbitrary rule. | **No dictatorship:** Neither a person nor a group may rule by force or arbitrarily. |
| g) The human rights set out in the Basic Law. | **Basic rights:** for example human dignity, equality of men and women, religious freedom, freedom of expression. |
| h) Antisemitic, racist or other acts motivated by contempt for human beings are incompatible with human dignity and contrary to the commitment. | **No contempt for human beings:** Anyone who attacks or demeans people because of their origin, religion or skin colour violates the basic order. This point was added in 2024. |

More on basic rights in the article **[Das Grundgesetz](/en/blog/das-grundgesetz/)** (in German).

## Part 3: Historical responsibility and the ban on wars of aggression

Since June 2024, you also commit:

**a) to Germany's special historical responsibility for the National Socialist regime of injustice and its consequences, in particular for the protection of Jewish life.**

Between 1933 and 1945, around six million European Jews were murdered. Germany derives from this the obligation to protect Jewish life and to oppose antisemitism. According to the federal application guidelines, the commitment also includes recognising Germany's special relationship with Israel. What this means for you is explained in the article **[Israel's right to exist and German citizenship](/en/blog/einbuergerung-existenzrecht-israel/)**.

**b) to peaceful coexistence between nations and the ban on waging a war of aggression.**

Article 26 of the Basic Law declares acts preparing a war of aggression unconstitutional. With this point, you recognise that states must resolve conflicts peacefully and that no state may invade another. Offices sometimes ask about Russia's war against Ukraine here to see whether you can apply the principle.

## Part 4: The declaration on activities

Finally, you declare that you do not pursue or support (and, in option 1, have not pursued or supported in the past) any activities that

- are directed against the free democratic basic order or against the existence or security of the Federation or a federal state,
- aim to unlawfully impair the conduct of office of constitutional bodies or their members, or
- endanger Germany's foreign interests through violence or preparations for violence.

With your signature, you confirm that you had the opportunity to ask questions, that you have understood the information leaflet and the content, and that your statements are true and complete.

## What happens if you make false statements?

The form expressly points out the consequences:

- **Refusal:** If there are factual indications that the commitment is untrue in substance, naturalisation is excluded under [Section 11 sentence 1 no. 1a StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__11.html).
- **Withdrawal:** If naturalisation was obtained through intentionally false or incomplete statements, it can be withdrawn within ten years.
- **Penalty:** Anyone who intentionally makes false or incomplete statements about essential requirements in order to obtain naturalisation by deception can be punished under [Section 42 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__42.html) with imprisonment of up to five years or a fine.

## The solemn declaration when you receive the certificate

Before you are handed your naturalisation certificate (Einbürgerungsurkunde), you also make a short solemn declaration under Section 16 StAG. In essence, you declare that you will respect the Basic Law and the laws of the Federal Republic of Germany and refrain from anything that could harm it. Many offices combine this with a naturalisation ceremony. What to do afterwards is explained in the article **[Einbürgerungsurkunde erhalten](/en/blog/einbuergerungsurkunde-erhalten-was-danach/)** (in German).

## Questions about the declaration of loyalty at your appointment

Many offices leave it at the signature. Some ask whether you have understood the content or hand out a questionnaire. Be prepared to explain the points above in your own words. You will find sample questions and model answers in the guide **[German citizenship interview questions](/en/blog/einbuergerung-gespraech-fragen-bekenntnis/)**.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do I sign the declaration of loyalty?

It varies: usually when you submit the application, sometimes at a later appointment or shortly before you are handed the certificate. Many offices require you to sign in the presence of the caseworker. Which other documents you need is listed in the **[Checkliste für den Einbürgerungsantrag](/en/blog/einbuergerung-antrag-checkliste-2026/)** (in German).

### Is the declaration of loyalty available in my language?

The Federal Ministry of the Interior has provided the federal states with translations of the commitment and an information sheet in numerous languages. Ask your office for them. You will usually have to sign the German version, so you should understand the German text.

### I used to be a member of an organisation and I am unsure. What should I do?

Do not choose option 1 too quickly. Describe the activity under option 3 or, if you have turned away from it, under option 2. If necessary, get advice beforehand from a migration advice service or a lawyer specialising in migration law.

### Do my children have to sign?

Children under 16 generally do not make the commitment themselves. For older children, the obligation applies as it does for adults. More in the article **[Einbürgerung mit Kindern](/en/blog/einbuergerung-mit-kindern/)** (in German).

### What happens if I do not understand something?

Ask before you sign. The form expressly confirms that you had the opportunity to ask questions. Do not sign anything you have not understood.
