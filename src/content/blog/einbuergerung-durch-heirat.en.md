**Naturalisation through marriage** does not happen automatically. If you are married to a German citizen, however, you can be naturalised under easier time requirements: as a rule, you must have been lawfully and habitually resident in Germany for three years and the marriage must have existed for two years. The general naturalisation requirements also apply, for example on identity, residence permit, livelihood, German language skills and civic knowledge.

The legal basis is [Section 9 of the Nationality Act (StAG)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__9.html). The rule applies equally to registered civil partnerships with a German citizen. A wedding therefore replaces neither the application nor the review by the nationality authority.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not personal legal advice. Your responsible nationality authority decides on the basis of your documents and your individual situation.

## Key points at a glance

- German citizenship does not arise from marriage alone.
- The easier route under Section 9 StAG normally requires **three years of lawful habitual residence in Germany** and **a marriage or civil partnership that has existed for two years**.
- Your marriage or civil partnership must still exist; a relationship that has already broken down generally does not support the easier route.
- B1 German and civic knowledge are normally still required.
- Identity, a suitable residence status, livelihood, loyalty to the constitution and any criminal offences are also checked.
- The application is filed with the locally responsible nationality authority. The exact document list can differ by case and authority.
- Naturalisation generally costs 255 euros per person. For a minor child without income who is naturalised together with a parent, the law sets 51 euros.

## What exactly does "naturalisation through marriage" mean?

The common expression sounds broader than the actual rule. Marriage does not give you a German passport, and you do not immediately become a German citizen. What is meant is **facilitated naturalisation for spouses and registered civil partners of German citizens**.

The main advantage concerns the residence period. For the general entitlement to naturalisation, [Section 10 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html) currently requires five years of lawful habitual residence. Section 9 StAG normally provides three years for spouses and registered partners of Germans if the relationship has already existed for two years.

The two periods are counted separately. Three years of marriage do not automatically replace three years of residence in Germany. Likewise, a long residence alone is not enough if the marriage has only existed for a few months.

## Which requirements apply besides the marriage?

Section 9 refers to the general requirements in Section 10(1) StAG. The authority therefore checks much more than your marriage certificate.

### Lawful residence and suitable status

You must be lawfully and habitually resident in Germany. Section 10 also requires a residence status that is suitable for naturalisation. Not every temporary residence permit is enough. What matters is the exact purpose of residence at the time of the decision.

If you are unsure, do not just compare your permit with general lists on the internet. Ask your nationality authority to confirm whether it fits your application. Our article **[Residence permits and citizenship](/en/blog/aufenthaltstitel-und-einbuergerung/)** gives a first overview.

### Identity and nationality

Your identity and current nationality must be established. This may require, for example, a valid passport, civil status certificates or other proof. Raise different spellings of names, missing certificates or unclear dates of birth early.

### Livelihood

In principle, you must be able to support yourself and dependent family members without benefits under Book II or Book XII of the Social Code. The law contains exceptions, including for certain full-time employees and for spouses or partners who live in a family unit with such a full-time employed person and a minor child.

Whether an exception applies to you depends on the specific facts. So do not just submit individual payslips; submit exactly the income and benefit documents your authority asks for.

### German language skills and the citizenship test

Marriage to a German citizen does not automatically exempt you from the language certificate or the citizenship test. As a rule, you must prove German at B1 level and knowledge of the legal and social order. Statutory exceptions and simplifications can apply, for example because of illness, disability, age or another recognised certificate.

See our overview of the **[German language requirements for citizenship](/en/blog/sprachanforderungen/)**. If you need the knowledge test, you can **[take the citizenship test without an integration course](/en/blog/einbuergerungstest-ohne-integrationskurs/)**.

### Declarations and criminal offences

The statutory requirements include commitments to the free democratic basic order and to Germany's special historical responsibility. Significant criminal convictions or statutory grounds for exclusion can stand in the way of naturalisation. Give complete and truthful information in the application; the authority carries out its own checks.

## Do three years of residence and two years of marriage always count automatically?

No. The periods are central conditions, but no guarantee of a positive decision. All other requirements must also be met. In addition, the marriage or civil partnership must genuinely continue.

The [official federal portal on naturalisation after marriage to a German citizen](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/de/leistung/99099002067002) points out that the relationship must be valid in Germany. The federal information portal on naturalisation also explains that the easier route does not apply if the relationship is already divorced or has broken down at the time of naturalisation. A marriage that only exists on paper is therefore not enough if the marital partnership has permanently ended.

Section 9 StAG allows a shorter residence period for reasons of public interest if the marriage or civil partnership has already existed for three years. This is not a general shortcut granted simply on request. Normally plan with the standard statutory periods and have any special case checked by the authority.

## Which documents should you prepare?

You will get the exact list from your local nationality authority. Typically you should have proof from these groups ready:

- identity and current nationality
- lawful residence and periods of residence
- valid marriage or registered civil partnership
- your partner's German citizenship
- shared household and current personal circumstances
- income, employment, housing and any benefits received
- B1 German
- citizenship test or a recognised substitute certificate
- civil status certificates and, if applicable, documents on children

If you married abroad, the authority may require translations, certifications or further proof that the marriage is valid. Only order paid translations once you have clarified which form is accepted. With the **[checklist for the citizenship application](/en/blog/einbuergerung-antrag-checkliste-2026/)** you can then sort your documents clearly.

## Naturalisation after marriage: the process in six steps

### 1. Find the responsible authority

The application generally goes to the nationality authority where you live. According to the [official federal questions and answers](https://www.einbuergerung.de/service.php), the city, district administration or borough office can help if this is unclear. Some authorities offer a digital application; others use their own forms or preliminary meetings.

### 2. Check the periods separately

Note since when you have been lawfully and habitually resident in Germany and on which date the marriage or civil partnership was concluded. Tell the authority openly about interruptions of residence and earlier changes of status instead of calculating the qualifying period yourself.

### 3. Request your personal document list

Ask for the current list for an application under Section 9 StAG. This way you avoid using only a general naturalisation list and forgetting the proof of marriage or of your partner's German citizenship.

### 4. Submit a complete application

Fill in all declarations carefully and upload clearly legible documents or submit them in the required form. Keep copies and proof of submission. Report later changes immediately, such as new contact details, a move, a change of job or a separation.

### 5. Answer follow-up questions and document changes

During processing, the authority may ask for current proof of income or other documents. Reply in an organised way and quote your file or reference number. Processing times vary considerably; there is no guaranteed short period nationwide.

### 6. Receive the certificate

Under [Section 16 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__16.html), naturalisation only takes effect when the naturalisation certificate is handed over. Before that, the solemn declaration required by law must be made. A positive message or an appointment notice alone does not yet make you a German citizen.

## Costs and naturalisation of children

Under [Section 38 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__38.html) the naturalisation fee is generally 255 euros. For a minor child without income of their own who is naturalised together with a parent, it is reduced to 51 euros. Individual costs can be added, for example for certificates, translations, the language exam or the citizenship test.

Under Section 9, minor children can be naturalised together with a parent under certain conditions, even if they have not yet lived lawfully in Germany for three years themselves. Custody, residence, language, age and the family situation play a role in the review. The article **[Naturalisation with children](/en/blog/einbuergerung-mit-kindern/)** helps you organise documents per person.

## Checklist before you apply

- Has my marriage or registered civil partnership existed for at least two years?
- Have I been lawfully and habitually resident in Germany for at least three years?
- Is my current residence permit suitable for naturalisation?
- Are identity, nationality and name spellings clearly documented?
- Do I have current proof of income, employment, housing and benefits?
- Do I have the B1 certificate and the test certificate, or have I clarified a possible exception?
- Have I used my authority's personal document list?
- Have I reported all changes since filing the application?

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### Do I automatically get German citizenship through marriage?

No. You must file a naturalisation application and meet all requirements. Under Section 9 StAG, marriage mainly allows a shorter required period of residence.

### Does my German spouse have to apply with me?

No, you apply for your own naturalisation. However, you need proof of the marriage and of your partner's German citizenship. The authority may ask for further information about your family life together.

### Do I have to prove B1 even though my spouse is German?

As a rule, yes. The general language requirements also apply to naturalisation under Section 9 StAG. Something different can only apply if a statutory exception or simplification applies.

### What happens in the event of separation or divorce during the procedure?

Inform the authority immediately. If the marital partnership has broken down, the easier route generally no longer applies. Section 9(2) contains a narrow special case after death or the final end of the relationship for parents with custody who live in a family unit with a minor German child from the relationship. Have this exception checked individually.

### May I keep my current citizenship?

Under German law, multiple citizenship is generally possible. The law of your country of origin may, however, have other consequences. You will find a detailed explanation under **[Dual citizenship and naturalisation](/en/blog/doppelte-staatsbuergerschaft/)**.

### Can I apply if I live abroad with my German partner?

The normal route under Section 9 requires lawful habitual residence in Germany. For people habitually resident abroad, Section 14 StAG contains a different, narrower discretionary rule. In that case, the authority of a former German place of residence is not automatically responsible.

## Next step

First check your two dates: the start of your lawful habitual residence in Germany and the start of the marriage or civil partnership. Then ask your nationality authority for the current document list specifically for an application under Section 9 StAG. This way you can see early which proof is still missing, before you order translations or submit the application.
