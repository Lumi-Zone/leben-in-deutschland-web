**To become a German citizen by naturalization you normally need five years of lawful residence in Germany, B1 German, a passed naturalization test (Leben in Deutschland questions), the ability to support yourself without Bürgergeld, a qualifying residence permit and no criminal convictions.** The legal basis is [§ 10 of the Nationality Act (StAG)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html). Since June 2024 Germany no longer requires you to give up your previous citizenship.

This guide explains each requirement in plain English. It is study information, not legal advice. Your local naturalization authority (Einbürgerungsbehörde) decides on your application.

## The requirements at a glance

| Requirement | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| 5 years of residence | Lawful, habitual residence in Germany for five years |
| Residence permit | Permanent residence, an EU Blue Card or another qualifying permit |
| German language | Level B1 (CEFR), usually proven with a certificate |
| Knowledge test | Naturalization test passed: at least 17 of 33 correct |
| Income | You can support yourself and your dependants without Bürgergeld or social assistance |
| No criminal convictions | The authority checks this; ask about any previous conviction |
| Commitment to the Basic Law | A signed declaration supporting the democratic constitutional order |
| Identity | Your identity and nationality must be clarified |

## 1. Five years of lawful residence

The standard period is **five years** of lawful, habitual residence in Germany. Before June 2024 it was eight years.

**Is German citizenship possible after 3 years?** Not on the basis of integration achievements anymore. A shortened three-year route for "special integration achievements" existed from June 2024, but it was abolished in October 2025. In the current text of § 10 StAG, paragraph 3 is marked as repealed ("weggefallen"). Articles that still promise "German citizenship in 3 years" for skilled workers are out of date.

A different rule applies to **spouses and registered partners of German citizens**: under [§ 9 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__9.html) they should normally be naturalized after three years of residence if the marriage or partnership has existed for two years. A spouse and minor children can also be naturalized together with the main applicant.

## 2. A qualifying residence permit

You need one of the following when you are naturalized:

- a permanent right of residence, for example a settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis)
- an **EU Blue Card**
- another residence permit for a purpose that the law does not exclude (temporary purposes such as studying and several humanitarian permits are excluded)

If you hold an EU Blue Card, read our guide on [moving from the EU Blue Card to German citizenship](/en/blog/eu-blue-card-german-citizenship/).

## 3. German at B1 level

The law requires "sufficient" German, defined as **level B1** of the Common European Framework of Reference. Most people prove this with a recognised certificate such as the DTZ (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer) at B1. Ask your authority which certificates or German school and university qualifications it accepts.

## 4. The naturalization test (Leben in Deutschland questions)

The knowledge requirement is "as a rule" proven with the **Einbürgerungstest**. It uses the same question catalogue as the Leben in Deutschland test:

- 33 questions in the exam: 30 general and 3 about your federal state
- **at least 17 correct answers** for naturalization
- 60 minutes, multiple choice, in German
- fee: €25 at an approved test centre

Preparation is free: the catalogue contains 300 general questions plus 10 questions per federal state. You can [practise all 300 questions with English translations](/en/fragen/) or [take a 33-question practice exam](/en/exam/). More detail: [how many correct answers you need](/en/blog/wie-viele-richtige-antworten-brauche-ich-einbuergerungstest/) and [how to book a test appointment](/en/blog/anmeldung-pruefung-schritte/).

People who cannot meet the language or knowledge requirement because of illness, disability or age are exempted.

## 5. Supporting yourself financially

You must be able to cover your living costs and those of dependent family members **without benefits under SGB II (Bürgergeld) or SGB XII (social assistance)**. The law contains narrow exceptions, for example for people who have worked full time for at least 20 of the last 24 months.

## 6. No criminal convictions

Applicants must not have been convicted of an unlawful act. Antisemitic, racist or other acts against human dignity are explicitly incompatible with naturalization. Ask the authority before applying if you have any previous conviction.

## 7. Commitment to the constitution

You sign a declaration that you support the free democratic basic order of the Basic Law (Grundgesetz) and acknowledge Germany's special historical responsibility for the Nazi regime and its consequences.

## Can I keep my current citizenship?

**Germany allows it.** The reform that came into force on 27 June 2024 accepts multiple citizenship, so you no longer have to give up your previous nationality for German law. Whether your **other country** lets you keep its citizenship depends on that country's law, so check with its embassy or consulate before you apply.

## Documents you usually need

Requirements differ by city, but a typical application includes:

- the application form of your local naturalization authority
- valid passport and residence permit
- birth certificate (and marriage certificate, if applicable), sometimes with certified translations
- B1 language certificate
- naturalization test certificate
- proof of income: employment contract, recent payslips, tax assessment or pension statements
- proof of health insurance
- registration certificate (Meldebescheinigung)

Your authority's own checklist is binding. Book an advice appointment early, because many authorities have long waiting lists.

## Costs

The naturalization fee is **€255 per adult** and **€51 for a minor child without own income** who is naturalized together with a parent ([§ 38 StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__38.html)). Budget extra for the €25 test, the language exam and any translations.

## How to apply: step by step

1. Check your years of residence and your permit type.
2. Get your B1 certificate.
3. Book and pass the naturalization test.
4. Collect documents and proof of income.
5. Submit your application to the naturalization authority of your city or district.
6. Wait for the decision and receive your naturalization certificate (Einbürgerungsurkunde).
