**The division and reunification of Germany can be understood through three milestones:** in 1949 two German states were founded. On 9 November 1989 the Berlin Wall opened. On 3 October 1990 Germany was reunified as a state. The fall of the Wall and reunification are therefore different events. The historical background is explained in the [Federal Agency for Civic Education's dossier on German division](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/).

If you discuss German history in your B1 or orientation course, you have to understand new words and dates at the same time. This article combines both: you put the events in order and then explain in simple sentences what changed for people. You do not need any prior historical knowledge.

## Key points at a glance

- **1949:** founding of the Federal Republic of Germany and the GDR.
- **1961:** construction of the Berlin Wall begins.
- **1989:** peaceful revolution and opening of the Wall.
- **1990:** reunification through the GDR's accession to the Federal Republic.
- Under SED rule the GDR was a dictatorship, despite the word "Democratic" in its name.
- Always remember the event that goes with a date: **founding the states, closing the border, opening the border, establishing unity.** The following sections explain these steps with sources.

## The timeline: which order should you know?

Start with a small overview. When revising, first cover the right-hand column and try to name the event yourself.

| Date | Event | Meaning in simple words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1949 | Two states founded | The Federal Republic and the GDR come into being. |
| 17 June 1953 | Uprising in the GDR | People protest; the uprising is ended by force. |
| 13 August 1961 | Construction of the Wall begins | The GDR leadership has the border to West Berlin sealed off. |
| Autumn 1989 | Peaceful revolution | Protests put SED rule under pressure. |
| 9 November 1989 | Fall of the Wall | Border crossings in Berlin are opened. |
| 18 March 1990 | Free Volkskammer election | People freely elect the GDR parliament. |
| 3 October 1990 | German reunification | The division into two states ends. |

You will find the evidence for each milestone in the relevant section. The table is a study aid for this topic, not a complete account of German history.

## 1949: Why were there two German states?

After the Second World War, the victorious powers administered Germany in occupation zones. Their political ideas increasingly drifted apart. A conflict known as the **Cold War** developed between the Western powers and the Soviet Union. In Germany this led to two different state systems.

The Federal Republic of Germany was founded on **23 May 1949**. The German Democratic Republic, the GDR, followed on **7 October 1949**. The Federal Republic grew out of the three western occupation zones, the GDR out of the Soviet zone. This is described by the [bpb in its section on the founding of two German states](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/43646/deutschlandpolitik-der-brd/).

In study materials you will also come across the abbreviation **BRD** for the Federal Republic at that time. Start by matching the terms like this: Federal Republic in the West, GDR in the East. Do not confuse the founding of the states with the building of the Wall: both states already existed before the Berlin Wall was built in 1961.

## What did GDR, SED and Stasi mean?

### A state's name does not explain its political system

GDR stands for **German Democratic Republic** (Deutsche Demokratische Republik, DDR). The word "Democratic" can be misleading when you study. Under SED rule, people could not replace the government through free elections. The party made the key political decisions. Criticism and opposition were suppressed.

The **Stasi** was the Ministry for State Security. It worked as an intelligence service and secret police and monitored people in order to secure SED rule. The [bpb provides a compact overview of the history of the GDR and its power structures](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/taegliche-dosis-politik/552731/geschichte-der-ddr/).

Keep three things apart when reading: **GDR refers to the state, SED to the ruling party, Stasi to the security apparatus.** Do not write all three terms on your flashcard as if they meant the same thing.

### How do you recognise the difference from democracy?

Ask about a political system: can people freely elect their government? May they disagree in public? Are their rights protected? Questions like these help you think beyond the mere name of a state. As a complement, our site has a [simple explanation of the Basic Law](/en/blog/das-grundgesetz/).

## 1953: What was the uprising of 17 June?

In June 1953, workers began protesting against higher work quotas. This developed into a broader uprising. The demands also concerned free elections and political change. Soviet troops, supported by GDR security forces, crushed the uprising by force. The [bpb explains the course of the uprising](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/ddr-kompakt/521494/der-volksaufstand-im-juni-1953/).

For your sense of time it is crucial: this protest took place **before the Wall was built**. It is not part of the peaceful revolution of 1989. So link "17 June" with "1953" and "uprising" instead of just learning the month.

## 1961: Why was the Berlin Wall built?

The GDR leadership wanted to stop large numbers of people from continuing to flee to the West via Berlin. Sealing off the border began on **13 August 1961**. Barriers and barbed wire developed into an extensive border system. The Wall separated West Berlin from East Berlin and from the surrounding GDR territory. The [bpb account of the history of the Wall](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/43695/geschichte-der-mauer/) explains the political reasons and the border closure.

When you look at a map, distinguish the **Berlin Wall** from the **border between the two German states**. Berlin is a city; the division also affected a whole country. The terms are connected but do not describe the same geographical line.

A suitable comprehension question is: "Was the Wall meant to stop people from leaving?" Explain your answer with the purpose of the border closure. This way you connect the year it was built with a cause instead of memorising an isolated number.

## 1989: How did the Wall fall?

In autumn 1989, more and more people demanded change. This included the Monday demonstrations in Leipzig and protests in other cities. At the same time, many GDR citizens left the country by other routes. The [bpb places the protest movement and departures within the peaceful revolution](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/43715/die-friedliche-revolution/).

On the evening of **9 November 1989**, the announcement of new travel rules led large numbers of people to go to the Berlin border crossings. There the crossings were eventually opened. The [bpb describes the events of 9 November 1989](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/taegliche-dosis-politik/515095/9-november-1989-33-jahre-mauerfall/).

**"The Wall fell" mainly means here: the border opened.** The expression does not mean that all walls were taken down that night. Nor does it mean that state reunification had already been completed. Further decisions were needed for the next political steps.

## 1990: What changed with reunification?

On **18 March 1990** the first free election to the Volkskammer took place. The Volkskammer was the GDR's parliament. This election was an important step on the way to unity, as the [bpb explains in its article on the 1990 Volkskammer election](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/43770/volkskammerwahl-1990/). At that point the GDR still existed.

On **3 October 1990** the GDR acceded to the Federal Republic of Germany. Brandenburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt and Thuringia became states of the Federal Republic. The two parts of Berlin together formed the state of Berlin. The [Unification Treaty published by the bpb, Articles 1 and 2](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/einigungsvertrag/44102/wirkung-des-beitritts/), sets out these changes and 3 October as the Day of German Unity.

Look for the five states mentioned on a map of Germany. If their location is not yet familiar to you, use our [overview of the federal states](/en/blog/die-16-bundeslaender/). This way you can connect history with today's place names.

## How to practise the topic in the B1 and orientation course

### Explain four words in your own sentences

Write one sentence for each term. The course is in German, so first use these German sentence starters and then try your own wording:

- **Teilung (division):** "Deutschland war geteilt, weil …" (Germany was divided because …)
- **Flucht (escape):** "Menschen verließen die DDR, um …" (People left the GDR in order to …)
- **Demonstration:** "Menschen gingen auf die Straße und forderten …" (People took to the streets and demanded …)
- **Wiedervereinigung (reunification):** "Seit dem 3. Oktober 1990 …" (Since 3 October 1990 …)

Check separately whether the historical content is right and whether your sentence is understandable. A grammatical mistake does not automatically mean you have misunderstood the event. Ask for feedback on both points in your course.

### Checklist for a short review

1. Draw the timeline from memory and leave uncertain places blank at first.
2. Add a verb to each milestone, for example "found" or "open".
3. Explain the difference between the fall of the Wall and reunification to another person.
4. Check your explanation against the relevant sections and sources.
5. Note one open question for the next lesson.

As a small self-check, mix three years: **1990, 1961, 1989**. Match them with reunification, the building of the Wall and the opening of the Wall, and give reasons for the order. This is a self-made study exercise, not an official exam task.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### Are the fall of the Wall and reunification the same thing?

No. The fall of the Wall refers to the opening of the border on 9 November 1989. State reunification followed on 3 October 1990. In between came, among other things, the free Volkskammer election and the preparation of accession.

### Why was the GDR not a democracy despite its name?

Under SED rule there was no free political competition through which the population could vote a government out. A state's name is therefore not sufficient evidence of democratic conditions. The free Volkskammer election of March 1990 already belongs to the period of upheaval.

### Do I need to know every exact date to understand it?

Start with the order and meaning of the events. Then add the exact dates from the table. This overview only covers German division and unity; it does not replace work on the other course and exam topics.

### How do I keep learning if German history words are hard for me?

Link a noun with a verb: "Teilung – teilen" (division – divide), "Gründung – gründen" (founding – found), "Öffnung – öffnen" (opening – open). Then write a short sentence with it. A translation can help you understand; afterwards also explain the connection in German.
