An **exemption from the citizenship test because of illness or disability** is possible if you cannot meet the required civic knowledge precisely because of your health impairment. Age-related reasons can also be taken into account. However, the exception does not apply automatically: your nationality authority examines your personal case and usually needs a meaningful medical certificate to do so.

So what matters is not a diagnosis alone or a particular degree of disability. It must be clear why you cannot meet the requirement. If possible, talk to your authority about the required evidence before you register for a test.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not personal legal advice. The binding decision is made by your responsible nationality authority after reviewing your documents.

## Key points at a glance

- Under Section 10(6) of the Nationality Act, the requirements for language skills and civic knowledge can be waived if they cannot be met because of illness, disability or age.
- A diagnosis alone does not automatically lead to an exemption from the citizenship test.
- The certificate should not only name the condition but explain clearly how it affects learning, understanding or taking the test.
- There is no nationwide automatic age limit for an exemption.
- If an exam under adapted conditions is possible, accessible test conditions may be an option for the "Leben in Deutschland" test.
- First clarify with the nationality authority whether an exception, another form of proof or an adapted test is the right route.

## When can the authority waive the citizenship test?

For an entitlement to naturalisation, applicants must generally prove knowledge of the legal and social order and living conditions in Germany. This is usually done by passing the citizenship test.

The statutory exception is in [Section 10(6) of the Nationality Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html). According to it, this requirement is waived if a person cannot meet it because of a physical, mental or psychological illness or disability, or because of age.

In practice, this means there must be a concrete link between the impairment and the inability to provide the proof of knowledge. Examples may include severe visual or hearing impairments, cognitive limitations, a mental illness or considerable motor impairments. Whether the requirements are met, however, cannot be decided across the board from a list of diagnoses.

The authority looks at the individual case. It may consider:

- which impairment exists,
- how long it is likely to last,
- what concrete effects it has on preparation and the test,
- whether a test under adapted conditions would be possible,
- which medical or other suitable evidence is available.

The Federal Government Commissioner for Migration, Refugees and Integration explains on the official portal [einbuergerung.de](https://www.einbuergerung.de/service.php) that a medical certificate can confirm that the test cannot be taken because of illness, disability or old age.

## An exemption does not mean any illness is enough

A chronic illness, a severe disability card or a care level can be important for the review. But this information does not automatically answer the decisive question: **Why can the person concerned not prove the civic knowledge because of this impairment?**

For example, someone with a physical disability who can study and complete a test with suitable aids may not meet the requirements for a full waiver of the knowledge requirement. Accessible exam conditions may then be the more suitable route.

Conversely, you should not play down a severe impairment. If learning, remembering, reading, writing, understanding or taking part in an exam is permanently and considerably impaired, the authority needs to know these concrete consequences. A bare statement such as "is ill" is usually less helpful than a clear medical description of the effects.

## What should the medical certificate say?

Not every authority uses an identical nationwide template. So ask your nationality authority whether it uses its own form or requires certain information. Only book the doctor's appointment once you know these requirements.

A meaningful certificate should typically show:

- who the person concerned is,
- what professional qualification the doctor has,
- which illness or disability exists,
- whether the impairment is temporary or likely to be permanent,
- what functional effects exist,
- why exactly this makes it impossible to learn or prove the required knowledge,
- whether, and with which adjustments, an exam might still be possible.

The diagnosis should therefore not stand without explanation. For the authority, the link between the medical findings and the test requirement is particularly important. At the same time, only have the documents prepared that are actually required, and send health data only via the secure channel provided.

## Illness, disability and age: where are the differences?

### Physical, mental or psychological illness

The law covers various kinds of illness. What matters is their concrete effect. A temporary illness, for example, may rather suggest postponing an appointment. A permanent severe illness, on the other hand, can justify an exception from the knowledge requirement if the requirement cannot be met because of it.

### Disability

A disability is also reviewed case by case. Section 10(6) StAG does not name a particular degree of disability as an automatic threshold. So do not rely on the severe disability card alone. Ask which additional medical explanation is needed.

### Age-related reasons

The law does not name a fixed age from which the citizenship test is automatically dropped. "Age-related" means instead that the requirement cannot be met because of the individual effects of age. Age alone and age-related inability are therefore not the same. The official naturalisation portal also names a medical certificate as possible proof here.

## Exemption or accessible test: two different routes

An **exemption** concerns the requirement in the naturalisation procedure. The nationality authority decides whether the proof of knowledge is waived. **Compensation for disadvantages**, by contrast, changes the conditions of an exam without removing the substantive requirements.

For the scaled "Leben in Deutschland" test, the BAMF has published its own [guidelines for accessible test conditions](https://www.bamf.de/SharedDocs/Anlagen/DE/Integration/Integrationskurse/Kurstraeger/Modellsaetze/richtlinien-barrierefreie-testbedingungen-dtz-lid.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4). They mention, among other things, visual impairment, blindness, deafness, hearing loss, reading difficulties and motor impairments. Depending on need, larger test materials, assistance, sign language interpreters or more time may be possible.

Important: this BAMF guideline refers to the **"Leben in Deutschland" (LiD) test**. LiD and the citizenship test are closely related but not identical organisationally in every respect. See our overview **[Leben in Deutschland test or citizenship test: the difference](/en/blog/unterschied-lid-test-einbuergerungstest/)**.

If you need an accessible LiD exam, contact the test centre early. According to the BAMF guidelines, the test centre applies to the Federal Office for the special conditions; complete documents should be there two months before the test date. So do not book just any appointment first; clarify the process in advance.

## How to proceed in five steps

### 1. Ask the responsible authority

Contact the nationality authority where you live. State briefly that, because of a health-related or age-related impairment, you would like to clarify how the proof under Section 10(6) StAG is assessed.

### 2. Get the requirements in writing

Ask for information about the certificate, form, how recent the evidence must be and how to submit it. This avoids a certificate that does not answer important questions from the authority.

### 3. Prepare the medical documents in a targeted way

Give the treating doctor the authority's requirements. Ask for a concrete description of the functional effects, not just the name of a diagnosis.

### 4. Claim the exception explicitly

Submit the certificate with your application or as instructed by the authority. State clearly that you are applying for an exception from the knowledge requirement under Section 10(1) sentence 1 number 7 in conjunction with subsection 6 StAG.

Our **[checklist for the citizenship application](/en/blog/einbuergerung-antrag-checkliste-2026/)** helps with the other documents.

### 5. Answer follow-up questions and wait for the decision

The authority may ask for additional information. Submit it on time. Do not register for the test too early if it is still open whether you need it or whether adapted conditions have to be organised.

## Does the exception also cover the B1 requirement?

Section 10(6) StAG covers both sufficient German language skills and civic knowledge. A health-related or age-related impairment can therefore also be relevant for the language requirement. Nevertheless, the authority reviews both requirements concretely; an exemption from the citizenship test does not automatically answer every question about the B1 certificate.

There are also further statutory rules and hardship situations for language and knowledge. You will find a general overview under **[German language requirements for citizenship](/en/blog/sprachanforderungen/)**. Discuss explicitly with the authority which exception you are claiming for which requirement.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### Is a severe disability card enough for an exemption?

Not automatically. The authority must be able to see why the specific disability prevents you from meeting the required knowledge. A more detailed medical certificate may be necessary for this.

### Is there a fixed age limit for the citizenship test?

No, Section 10(6) StAG does not name a fixed age limit. What matters is whether you cannot meet the requirement because of age. The authority reviews this individually.

### Can my GP write the certificate?

That depends on the requirements of the responsible authority and your condition. Ask beforehand whether a GP's certificate is enough or a specialist's assessment is expected.

### Do I first have to fail the test despite my illness?

The law does not require an unsuccessful attempt at the test as a general condition for the exception. So clarify the evidence with the authority before registering.

### Can I get more time instead of an exemption?

For the "Leben in Deutschland" test, special accessible test conditions are possible under the BAMF guidelines. Which adjustment is suitable is reviewed individually and must be applied for early via the test centre.

### Does a test exemption simplify the whole naturalisation application?

No. The exception concerns certain knowledge or evidence. The other naturalisation requirements are still reviewed. Our article **[Naturalisation: process and requirements](/en/blog/german-citizenship-requirements/)** shows the overall process.

## Next step

First write three short questions to your nationality authority: Which evidence does it require for Section 10(6) StAG? Is there a certificate form? And should an exemption or an accessible LiD test be considered in your case? Only then should you commission medical documents or book a test date.
