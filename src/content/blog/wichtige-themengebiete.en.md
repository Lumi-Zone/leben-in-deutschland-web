The "Leben in Deutschland" test is about Germany, politics, history and everyday life. The 300 general questions cover three main topic areas.

## 1. Politics in a democracy
This is a very important part of the test. It covers:
*   **Fundamental rights**: which rights does the Basic Law guarantee? (e.g. freedom of expression, equality).
*   **Constitutional bodies**: what does the Bundestag do? Who elects the Federal Chancellor? What are the Federal President's tasks?
*   **Electoral system**: how do elections work in Germany? What are the first and second votes?
*   **Parties**: what role do parties play in a democracy?

## 2. History and responsibility
This area mainly deals with 20th-century German history:
*   **National Socialism**: Adolf Hitler's dictatorship, the Second World War and the Holocaust.
*   **Post-war period**: the division of Germany, the founding of the FRG and the GDR.
*   **Reunification**: the fall of the Wall in 1989 and reunification in 1990.
*   **European integration**: the founding of the EU and Germany's role in Europe.

## 3. People and society
This is about everyday living together:
*   **Social system**: how do health insurance, pension insurance and unemployment benefit work?
*   **Education**: how is the school system structured?
*   **Living together**: religious diversity, equality of men and women, and tolerance.

## Conclusion
Do not just memorise answers. Also try to understand why an answer is correct. Then you will find the right answer more easily, even when you are nervous.

## Quick summary
- The citizenship test mainly checks democracy, history and society.
- In addition there are 3 questions about your federal state.
- If you understand the topics, you need to memorise less.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Which topics are particularly important?
Fundamental rights, democracy, elections, German history and life in society are very important.

### Do I have to know all topics perfectly?
No, but you should understand the basic terms and practise regularly with the original questions.

### Why do everyday examples help?
Examples make terms easier. This helps you remember what democracy, fundamental rights or the welfare state mean.
