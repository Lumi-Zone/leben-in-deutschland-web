Elections are the heart of democracy. In Germany there are a few special features you should know for the citizenship test.

## Who may vote?
In Bundestag elections, all German citizens aged 18 and over may vote. In local elections, EU citizens living in Germany can often vote as well.

## The electoral system
Every voter has two votes in the Bundestag election:
1.  **First vote (Erststimme)**: with it you vote directly for a candidate in your constituency (direct mandate). The candidate with the most votes wins the constituency. Since the 2023 electoral reform, however, they only enter the Bundestag if the seat is covered by their party's second votes.
2.  **Second vote (Zweitstimme)**: this is the more important vote! With it you vote for a party. The second vote decides how many seats a party receives in the Bundestag overall.

## The 5% threshold
So that not too many small parties sit in parliament and block decisions, there is the 5% threshold. A party must receive at least **5% of the second votes** to enter the Bundestag (or win 3 direct mandates).

## Secret and free
Elections in Germany are:
*   **Universal**: all citizens may vote.
*   **Direct**: you elect the representatives directly (no electors).
*   **Free**: nobody may be forced.
*   **Equal**: every vote counts the same.
*   **Secret**: nobody may know whom you voted for (voting booth).

## Quick summary
- Elections are an important part of democracy in Germany.
- In the Bundestag election, the first and second votes matter.
- The election is free, equal, secret and universal.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Who may vote in the Bundestag election?
German citizens may vote if they are at least 18 years old and meet the other requirements.

### What is the second vote?
With the second vote you vote for a party. It is particularly important for the distribution of seats in the Bundestag.

### Why is the vote secret?
Nobody should be able to check whom you voted for. This protects the freedom of the vote.
