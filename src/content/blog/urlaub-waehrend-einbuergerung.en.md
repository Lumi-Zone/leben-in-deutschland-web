The naturalisation process can take months or even years. Many applicants wonder: am I stuck in Germany during this time?

## Short trips are no problem
In principle, you may of course go on holiday or visit relatives. A normal holiday (e.g. 2–3 weeks) does not endanger your citizenship application.

## Be careful with long stays
It becomes critical if you leave the country for **more than 6 months** in a row.
*   **Residence permit**: your settlement permit or residence permit can expire if you are away for too long. No valid permit, no naturalisation!
*   **Centre of life**: for naturalisation, your centre of life must be in Germany. If you spend more time abroad than here, you may no longer meet this requirement.

## Duty to report changes
If you move (even within Germany) or go abroad for a longer period, you must tell the naturalisation authority. Post that cannot be delivered often leads to the file being put "on hold" or the application being refused.

**Conclusion**: enjoy your holiday, but keep an eye on the 6-month limit and empty your letterbox!

## Quick summary
- Short holidays are usually no problem during the naturalisation process.
- Long stays abroad can affect your residence permit and your centre of life.
- Watch out for post from the authority and report important changes quickly.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### May I go on holiday during the naturalisation process?
Yes, a normal holiday is usually possible. What matters is that you remain reachable and do not miss any deadlines.

### When can a stay abroad become a problem?
Very long stays can become problematic. Especially for more than 6 months, you should check beforehand whether your residence permit is affected.

### Do I have to report my holiday to the naturalisation authority?
Short trips often do not have to be reported. If you are away for longer, move or have a new address, you should inform the authority.
