If you want to **practise the citizenship test online**, you need more than a random collection of questions. What matters is a clear study process: first understand the official questions, then revise your own mistakes and finally train under real exam conditions.

The test is easy to plan for because the topics and the question framework are known. That is exactly why it pays to study in a structured way rather than simply clicking through new questions every day.

## The key facts about the 2026 citizenship test
In the test you get **33 multiple-choice questions**. Each question has four answer options, one of which is correct. You have **60 minutes**.

To prove civic knowledge for naturalisation, you normally need **at least 17 correct answers**. The questions come from the areas of democracy, history, responsibility, society and your federal state.

What matters personally when practising:
- 300 general questions
- 10 questions about your federal state
- sample papers and exam simulations

The complete catalogue also contains the state questions for all 16 federal states. For your exam, however, only the questions for the state where your main residence is registered count.

## Why practising online is so effective
Studying online has one big advantage: you see immediately which questions you know well and which are still shaky. This makes your preparation measurable.

Three features are particularly helpful:
1. **Mistake list**: every wrongly answered question is revised in a targeted way.
2. **Federal state selection**: you do not just train in general but for where you live.
3. **Exam simulation**: you practise 33 questions in one go and get used to the format.

This way you avoid the most common study mistake: clicking through lots of questions once without really consolidating the difficult ones.

## The 14-day preparation plan
A compact plan is often enough if you study regularly. What matters is not the length of a single session but repetition.

### Days 1 to 3: Get an overview
Start with the general questions. The goal is not perfection yet but orientation: which terms do you already know? Which topics feel difficult?

Mark questions on these topics straight away:
- Bundestag, Bundesrat and federal government
- fundamental rights and duties
- elections and parties
- German history
- federal states and federalism

### Days 4 to 7: Revise mistakes in a targeted way
Now your mistake list becomes more important than new questions. Revise every wrongly answered question at least twice.

For difficult questions, note the reason:
- term confused
- question read too quickly
- institution assigned wrongly
- historical date not certain

This short analysis saves more time than pure memorisation.

### Days 8 to 10: Train the federal state questions
Many learners put off the state questions until shortly before the test. That is risky, because these questions count in the real test.

Select your federal state and revise the state questions until you can answer them without much thought. Pay particular attention to the state parliament, state government, coat of arms, Minister President and regional features.

### Days 11 to 13: Exam simulation
Now practise in test mode: 33 questions, ideally without interruption. Do not stop after a wrong answer; work through the whole test as on exam day.

After each simulation, check:
- Did I get at least 17 correct answers?
- Which topics are still causing mistakes?
- Am I losing points through carelessness?
- Do I stay under 60 minutes?

If you are consistently above the pass mark in several simulations, that is a good sign. It is safer not just to scrape through but to regularly score well above 17 points.

### Day 14: Light revision instead of stress
On the last day you should not open up any completely new topics. Revise your mistake list, the state questions and two or three topics you still feel unsure about.

Go to bed early, have your ID ready and plan your journey. A calm exam starts the day before.

## Common mistakes when studying online
Many candidates study hard but lose points to avoidable patterns.

### Memorising only the correct answers
This helps in the short term but is unreliable. Better: always read the wrong options too and understand why they are wrong.

### Selecting your federal state too late
If you practise with the wrong federal state, you waste valuable study time. Set your state right at the beginning.

### Not simulating real tests
Practising single questions is important. Even so, you should take several complete simulations before your appointment. This shows you whether concentration, speed and knowledge work together.

### Not revising mistakes
A wrongly answered question is not a problem. It only becomes one if you get it wrong again next time. That is why a mistake list is your most powerful tool.

## Quick summary
- The citizenship test has 33 questions, 60 minutes and requires at least 17 correct answers for naturalisation.
- Practising online is especially effective if you combine the official questions, federal state questions, a mistake list and exam simulations.
- A 14-day plan with daily revision is better than long, unstructured study sessions.
- Shortly before the appointment, analysing mistakes, simulating tests and staying calm matter more than new topics.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Can I take the citizenship test completely online?
No. You can prepare online, but the exam itself takes place at an approved test centre.

### How many questions do I need to practise online?
For your preparation you should master the 300 general questions and the 10 questions for your federal state.

### Is it enough to take sample tests only?
Not quite. Sample tests show where you stand, but they do not replace the targeted revision of wrong answers.

### When am I ready for the test?
A good sign is when you score clearly above 17 correct answers in several exam simulations in a row and can explain your mistakes.

### Should I still study a lot on the last day?
No. Only revise your mistake list, the state questions and a few uncertain topics. New content shortly before the appointment usually only increases stress.
