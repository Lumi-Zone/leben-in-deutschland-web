An available date is often the slowest part of the process. Especially in large cities, exam places are booked up quickly. Good planning often saves you time.

## Leben in Deutschland test or citizenship test: where do I book the date?

Both exams use the same question catalogue, but the route to a date is different:

- **"Leben in Deutschland" (LiD) test:** it is the final test of the orientation course within the integration course. Your course provider usually organises the date.
- **Citizenship test (Einbürgerungstest):** you register yourself with an approved test centre, often an adult education centre (VHS). The fee is 25 euros.

The guide [Leben in Deutschland test and citizenship test](/en/blog/unterschied-lid-test-einbuergerungstest/) explains the difference.

## Finding dates near you

You can find test centres near you through the [BAMF information on the citizenship test](https://www.bamf.de/DE/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html). Ask several centres about available dates and a waiting list. You can only book online if the test centre offers this; you always take the test itself on site.

## Why availability varies so much
Demand differs greatly by region. While metropolitan areas often have longer lead times, places are sometimes available at shorter notice in surrounding towns.

Typical reasons:
- high numbers of applications
- limited exam capacity
- fixed exam cycles instead of ongoing dates

## 5 tips for faster dates
1. **Start early**: do not leave registration until the last month.
2. **Check several test centres**: include neighbouring towns.
3. **Bring complete documents**: missing documents often cost you a new appointment.
4. **Be flexible about time and weekday**: this increases your chance of free places.
5. **Clarify questions directly**: unclear details lead to delays in registration.

## Documents often requested at registration
- valid ID card or passport
- exam fee (usually 25 euros)
- depending on the centre, further registration details or forms

Important: the exact list may differ by test centre. Always check the instructions of the responsible centre in advance.

## Timing within the naturalisation process
The test is only one part of the overall procedure. If you secure the certificate early, the authority can later continue without this bottleneck.

Recommendation:
- plan the test date early
- allow for the time it takes to get the result
- prepare the application and documents in parallel

## Quick summary
- Booking a date for the citizenship test is often the biggest time factor before applying.
- Regional flexibility and complete documents increase your chance of earlier exam places.
- Planning the test, documents and application in parallel often saves several months.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### When should I register at the latest?
As early as possible. In many regions several weeks or months of lead time are realistic.

### Can I take the test in another city?
In many cases yes, if the test centre accepts you and the organisational requirements are met.

### What happens if I cannot find a date?
Regularly check alternative test centres, places that become free at short notice and neighbouring regions.

### Does a late test delay my citizenship application?
Often yes. Without the test certificate, processing may stall depending on the case.
