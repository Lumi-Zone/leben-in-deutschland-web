If you want to live in Germany, you should speak the language and understand the culture. That is what the integration course is for. But what exactly happens there?

## Structure of the course
The integration course has two parts:
1.  **Language course**: 600 lessons (up to 900 in special courses). The goal is language level B1.
2.  **Orientation course**: 100 lessons. This part is about "life in Germany" (Leben in Deutschland).

## Content of the language course
Here you learn the vocabulary for all important areas of everyday life:
*   work and profession
*   shopping and housing
*   health and hygiene
*   parenting and school
*   media and leisure

## The orientation course
This part prepares you specifically for the "Leben in Deutschland" test. Topics are:
*   **Legal order**: the Basic Law, fundamental rights, the tasks of the police.
*   **History**: National Socialism, the division of Germany, reunification.
*   **Culture**: public holidays, manners, religious diversity.

## Completion
The course ends with two exams: the "German Test for Immigrants" (Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer, DTZ) and the "Leben in Deutschland" test. If you pass both, you receive the "Integration Course Certificate". For naturalisation it can serve as proof of German skills (B1) and knowledge about Germany. Since the 2024 reform it no longer shortens the waiting time: as a rule, five years of residence apply to everyone.

## Quick summary
- The integration course consists of a language course and an orientation course.
- In the language course you learn German for everyday life, work and dealing with authorities.
- In the orientation course you learn about politics, history, law and society.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### How much time should I plan per week?
It depends on the course model. In addition to lessons, short revision sessions at home help.

### What is the orientation course?
The orientation course is the part about Germany: politics, history, law, values and everyday life.

### Does the integration course help with naturalisation?
Yes. It can help with the language certificate and knowledge about Germany if you achieve the required results.
