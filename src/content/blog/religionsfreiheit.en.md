Article 4 of the Basic Law guarantees **freedom of faith and conscience**.

## What does that mean?
*   You may have any religion (Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, etc.).
*   You may also have **no** religion (atheism).
*   You may change your religion (conversion).
*   The state is neutral. There is no state church.

## Limits
Freedom of religion ends where the law begins. You may not commit crimes in the name of religion or violate the rights of others. For example, the Basic Law ranks above religious rules.

In Germany, people of many different religions live together peacefully. Tolerance is the most important precondition for this.

## Quick summary
- In Germany everyone may have a religion or no religion.
- The state may not disadvantage people because of their religion.
- The freedom ends where laws and the rights of others are violated.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### May I choose my religion freely?
Yes. You may believe what you want. You may also have no religion.

### Does the state have to treat all religions equally?
Yes. The state must remain neutral and may not favour or disadvantage anyone because of religion.

### Are there limits to freedom of religion?
Yes. For example, no laws may be broken and no other people may be endangered.
