**In short:** The word "Israel" does not appear in the law. But anyone who wants to be naturalised must commit to **Germany's special historical responsibility for the National Socialist regime of injustice and its consequences, in particular for the protection of Jewish life**. According to the application guidelines of the Federal Ministry of the Interior, this also includes recognising Germany's special relationship with Israel. A court in Regensburg has ruled that anyone who rejects Israel's right to exist cannot be naturalised. According to the reports available to us, only **Saxony-Anhalt** required a **separate written commitment on Israel**.

What this means for you: expect that offices may ask about your attitude towards Israel, and know the difference between permitted criticism of a government and rejecting a state's right to exist.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not legal advice. Practice in the federal states changes. The information on the federal states is based on reports up to August 2025; what your naturalisation office (Einbürgerungsbehörde) tells you is what counts.

## Key points

- Since June 2024, [Section 10(1) sentence 1 no. 1a StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html) requires a commitment to Germany's historical responsibility and to the protection of Jewish life. Israel is not named in the law; the governing coalition at the time deliberately left it out.
- According to reports, the application guidelines of the Federal Ministry of the Interior (sent to the federal states at the end of May 2025) include recognition of the **special and close relationship with Israel** and describe Israel's security and right to exist as part of Germany's raison d'état (Staatsräson).
- Since a decree of 29 November 2023, **Saxony-Anhalt** has required an express written declaration on Israel's right to exist. By March 2025, ten naturalisations had been refused there for this reason.
- According to a survey by the Protestant Press Service (Evangelischer Pressedienst) in August 2025, most other federal states consider a separate declaration unnecessary because current law is sufficient.
- On 7 October 2024, the **Administrative Court of Regensburg** dismissed the claim of a Palestinian who had said: "There is no Israel." The ruling is final but disputed among lawyers.
- The naturalisation test includes questions about Israel, for example about its founding and about Germany's special responsibility.

## What does the law say?

Since the reform of nationality law in June 2024, applicants for naturalisation must commit to Germany's special historical responsibility for the National Socialist regime of injustice and its consequences, in particular for the protection of Jewish life, as well as to peaceful coexistence between nations and the ban on wars of aggression.

An express commitment to the State of Israel is not in the law. Politicians of the then "traffic light" coalition (Ampel-Koalition) stated that Israel had deliberately not been named. On 2 February 2024, on the initiative of North Rhine-Westphalia and Schleswig-Holstein, the Bundesrat adopted a resolution according to which a credible commitment to Israel's right to exist should be a requirement for naturalisation. Such a resolution is a political demand, not a law.

What exactly the commitment covers is explained in the **application guidelines of the Federal Ministry of the Interior** (Anwendungshinweise). According to reports on the version sent out in May 2025, this includes recognising Germany's special and close relationship with Israel. Application guidelines direct the authorities in practice, but they do not replace a law.

## What applies in the federal states?

| Federal state | Practice according to reports (up to August 2025) |
| --- | --- |
| Saxony-Anhalt | Separate **written declaration** on Israel's right to exist shortly before naturalisation (decree of 29 November 2023). Anyone who does not sign is not naturalised. |
| Brandenburg | In July 2025, the Ministry of the Interior announced that it would give recognition of Israel's right to exist more weight in the procedure. According to reports, a separate written declaration was not planned. |
| North Rhine-Westphalia | Calls for a uniform nationwide rule that sets out recognition of Israel's right to exist more clearly as a requirement. |
| Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania | Recognition is said to already be part of the naturalisation requirements; a separate declaration is not necessary. |
| Schleswig-Holstein | Anyone who pursues antisemitic aims and denies Israel's right to exist cannot be naturalised under federal law as it stands. |
| Thuringia, Bremen | The current rules are sufficient; Bremen refers to an information sheet on how to interpret the commitment. |
| Bavaria | The Administrative Court of Regensburg (see below) counts recognition of Israel's right to exist as part of the commitment. |

We had no separate information on the remaining federal states. In Lower Saxony there is no separate Israel declaration; however, some offices expressly address Germany's role towards Judaism or ask written questions on the historical responsibility. More in the article **[Naturalisation in Lower Saxony: how offices check the commitment](/en/blog/einbuergerung-niedersachsen-bekenntnis-befragung/)**.

### The declaration from Saxony-Anhalt

The decree from Magdeburg recommends a declaration in which applicants expressly recognise Germany's special responsibility for the State of Israel and Israel's right to exist, condemn antisemitic activities and confirm that they do not pursue any activities directed against Israel's existence. If it later turns out that someone made the declaration by fraudulent deception, withdrawal of the naturalisation is to be examined.

## The Regensburg ruling

On **7 October 2024**, the Administrative Court of Regensburg (case no. RO 9 K 24.782) ruled on the claim of a stateless Palestinian who had been recognised as a refugee in 2016 and had applied for naturalisation in 2022.

**The case:** In the interview with the naturalisation office, he was asked whether he recognised Israel as an independent state. According to the record, he answered: "There is no Israel. There are Jews, but not Israel as a country." He later asked for a correction and wrote that he recognised the State of Israel following the Oslo peace process and had only wanted to criticise the occupation. The office considered the correction tactical and refused his application in March 2024.

**The decision:** The court dismissed the claim. Anyone who rejects Israel's right to exist cannot be naturalised, regardless of whether this attitude is motivated by antisemitism or "only" by anti-Zionism. The court read the clause on historical responsibility as including recognition of Israel, because the founding of Israel was a consequence of the Holocaust. It was not persuaded by the later correction. The ruling is final.

**The criticism:** In the [Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/die-idee-der-staatsrason-im-neuesten-deutschen-recht/), the ruling is criticised as wrong: the legislature deliberately chose not to include a commitment to Israel, and the court turns the political idea of raison d'état into a requirement for naturalisation. Whether other courts will follow the Regensburg line is open.

## Israel in the naturalisation test

Since 2024, the question catalogue has included questions on Jewish life, antisemitism and Israel. You should be able to answer these with confidence:

- **[Question 288](/en/frage/288/):** What is the basis of Germany's special responsibility for Israel? Correct: the National Socialist crimes against Jews.
- **[Question 184](/en/frage/184/):** On what legal basis was the State of Israel founded? Correct: a resolution of the United Nations.
- **[Question 111](/en/frage/111/):** Which actions relating to the State of Israel are prohibited in Germany? Correct: publicly calling for the destruction of Israel.
- **[Question 149](/en/frage/149/):** What is an example of antisemitic behaviour? Correct: denying the Holocaust.

## Criticising Israel: what is allowed?

Freedom of expression also applies to applicants for naturalisation. You may criticise the policies of the Israeli government, just as you may criticise the policies of any other government. It becomes problematic when:

- Israel's **right to exist is denied** or its destruction is called for,
- **Jewish people as a whole** are held responsible for Israel's policies,
- the Holocaust is denied or trivialised,
- violence against Jewish people is justified.

Antisemitically motivated offences have particularly serious consequences for naturalisation: they count even if the sentence is so small that it would otherwise be disregarded. More in the article **[When naturalisation is excluded](/en/blog/einbuergerung-ausschlussgruende-antisemitismus-mehrehe/)**.

## If you are asked about Israel in the interview

A clear, honest answer has three parts: the historical responsibility, recognition of the right to exist and, if you wish, the distinction between the state and government policy. For example (in German, as you will usually answer in German):

> Deutschland hat im Nationalsozialismus etwa sechs Millionen Jüdinnen und Juden ermordet. Daraus folgt eine besondere Verantwortung für jüdisches Leben und für Israel. Ich erkenne das Existenzrecht Israels an. Man darf in Deutschland die Politik einer Regierung kritisieren, aber nicht das Existenzrecht eines Staates bestreiten oder Jüdinnen und Juden dafür verantwortlich machen.

*In English:* Under National Socialism, Germany murdered about six million Jews. This gives rise to a special responsibility for Jewish life and for Israel. I recognise Israel's right to exist. In Germany you may criticise a government's policies, but you may not deny a state's right to exist or hold Jewish people responsible for those policies.

Avoid answers that leave the right to exist open, such as "That is a complicated political question" without a clear statement. The Regensburg case shows that offices and courts take the first recorded answer very seriously. You will find more practice questions and sample answers in our guide **[German citizenship interview questions](/en/blog/einbuergerung-gespraech-fragen-bekenntnis/)**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to sign an Israel declaration in Bavaria, Berlin or North Rhine-Westphalia?

According to the reports available to us, a separate written Israel declaration was only mandatory in Saxony-Anhalt. Everywhere, however, the commitment to historical responsibility applies, and according to the federal application guidelines it covers the special relationship with Israel. Ask your office for the current forms.

### I have family in Palestine or Lebanon. May I give my opinion on the Middle East conflict?

Yes. You may name suffering and criticise government policy. What matters is that you do not deny Israel's right to exist, do not justify violence and do not hold Jewish people as a whole responsible.

### What happens if I sign the commitment but think differently?

A commitment that does not reflect your conviction is untrue in substance. If there are factual indications of this, naturalisation is excluded. If naturalisation was obtained through intentionally false statements, it can be withdrawn within ten years. Knowingly false statements can also be a criminal offence.
