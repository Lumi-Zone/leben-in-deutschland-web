Parties take part in forming the political will of the people. Here are the most important parties, which are often represented in the Bundestag:

## The main parties
*   **SPD (Social Democratic Party of Germany)**: a traditional workers' party. Topics: social justice, workers' rights, solidarity. Colour: red.
*   **CDU/CSU (Christian Democratic Union / Christian Social Union)**: conservative parties (the "Union"). Topics: economy, security, Christian values, family. The CSU only stands in Bavaria. Colour: black.
*   **Alliance 90/The Greens (Bündnis 90/Die Grünen)**: topics: environmental protection, climate change, sustainability, social diversity. Colour: green.
*   **FDP (Free Democratic Party)**: topics: liberal economic policy, civil liberties, digitalisation, less state. Colour: yellow (or blue and yellow).
*   **AfD (Alternative for Germany)**: right-wing populist, critical of migration and the EU. Colour: blue.
*   **The Left (Die Linke)**: topics: socialism, redistribution of wealth, anti-capitalism. Colour: red (dark red).

There are many other small parties. Germany has a multi-party system, so it is usually **coalitions** (alliances of two or more parties) that govern, because one party rarely has a majority on its own.

## Quick summary
- Parties gather political ideas and put forward candidates.
- Citizens vote for parties, for example in Bundestag and state elections.
- What matters for the test: parties are part of democracy.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Do I have to memorise party programmes?
No. For the citizenship test it is more important that you understand the role of parties in a democracy.

### Why are there several parties?
People have different opinions. Parties represent different political goals.

### What does opposition mean?
The opposition consists of parties in parliament that are not part of the government. They scrutinise and criticise the government.
