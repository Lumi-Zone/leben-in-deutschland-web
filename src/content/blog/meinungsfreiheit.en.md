"Every person shall have the right freely to express and disseminate their opinions in speech, writing and pictures." (Article 5 of the Basic Law).

## No censorship
In Germany there is no censorship by the state. Newspapers, television and the internet may report critically on the government. This is called **freedom of the press**. It is an important feature of a democracy.

## Criticism is allowed
You may say: "I think the government is bad" or "I don't like law X". You may also demonstrate.

## The limits
Freedom of expression is not unlimited. The following are prohibited:
*   **Insult**: you may not abuse anyone personally.
*   **Defamation / lies**: you may not spread lies about others in order to harm them.
*   **Incitement to hatred (Volksverhetzung)**: you may not stir up hatred against groups of the population or call for violence.
*   **Symbols of unconstitutional organisations**: swastikas or the Hitler salute are banned in Germany.

Freedom of expression protects democracy, but it does not protect hatred and incitement.

## Quick summary
- In Germany people may freely state their opinion.
- Criticism of politics, the state and parties is allowed.
- Insults, incitement to hatred and calls for violence are not protected.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### May I criticise the government?
Yes. Criticism of the government, parties and authorities is allowed in Germany.

### Does freedom of expression mean everything is allowed?
No. Insults, threats or incitement to hatred can be criminal offences.

### Why is freedom of expression important in the citizenship test?
It shows that Germany is a democracy. People may hold different opinions and discuss them publicly.
