In a democracy, citizens do not only have freedoms but also duties towards the community.

## Your rights
*   **Freedom of movement**: you may live wherever you want (within Germany/the EU).
*   **Freedom of assembly**: you may demonstrate and assemble peacefully.
*   **Freedom of occupation**: you may choose your profession freely.
*   **Right to vote**: as a citizen you may vote and stand for election.

## Your duties
There is no "compulsory voting" (voting is voluntary), but there are other duties:
*   **Duty to pay taxes**: anyone who earns money must pay taxes. This is how the state finances schools, roads and the police.
*   **Compulsory schooling**: children must go to school (usually from age 6 for at least 9–10 years). Homeschooling is not allowed in Germany.
*   **Obeying the law**: of course everyone must comply with the Criminal Code and other laws.
*   **Duty to register**: when you move house, you must register your new address at the residents' registration office (Bürgeramt).

The balance of rights and duties ensures that living together works.

## Quick summary
- Citizens have important rights, for example freedom of expression and the right to vote.
- There are also duties, for example obeying the law and paying taxes.
- Many questions in the citizenship test check this basic idea.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Which rights are particularly important?
Important ones include freedom of expression, freedom of religion, equality and the right to vote for German citizens.

### What duties are there in Germany?
Everyone must obey the law. Depending on the situation, there are also duties such as compulsory schooling or paying taxes.

### Is voting compulsory in Germany?
No. Voting is a right, not a duty. Nobody has to vote, but every vote can matter politically.
