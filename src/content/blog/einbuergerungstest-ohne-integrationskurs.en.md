Yes, you can take the **citizenship test without an integration course**. You do not have to attend an integration course or a special preparation course beforehand. You register directly with an approved test centre, prepare on your own and, once you pass, receive a certificate for the naturalisation authority.

The law is clear on this point: under [Section 10(5) of the Nationality Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html), preparation courses are offered, but attending them is not compulsory. A course can help you learn. It is not, however, a requirement for taking the citizenship test.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not personal legal advice. Whether you need the test certificate in your specific naturalisation procedure is decided by your responsible nationality authority.

## Key points at a glance

- You may take the citizenship test without first attending an integration course.
- You arrange the date with an approved test centre, often an adult education centre (Volkshochschule) or another approved course provider.
- Under the current Naturalisation Test Ordinance, taking part costs 25 euros.
- You answer 33 questions in 60 minutes. You need at least 17 correct answers to pass.
- To prepare, you can use the official question catalogue and the BAMF Online Test Centre.
- The passed test proves civic knowledge. It does not replace any German language certificate that may be required.

## Why is no integration course required?

For naturalisation, applicants usually have to prove knowledge of the legal and social order and living conditions in Germany. This is mostly done with a passed citizenship test. The required proof is therefore the **test result**, not prior attendance of a course.

The current [BAMF registration form for the citizenship test](https://www.bamf.de/SharedDocs/Anlagen/DE/Integration/Integrationskurse/Kurstraeger/Testverfahren/635.005-teilnehmermeldebogen-einbuergerungstest-pdf.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=9) also explicitly covers both options: participants state whether or not they have attended a naturalisation course. On the same form you can also tick whether a citizenship application has already been filed.

In practice, this means you can generally plan the test as a single exam and do not have to start a full integration course first. Still, clarify with your nationality authority which proof it expects in your case. There are statutory exceptions, and you may already have a recognised certificate.

## Integration course, orientation course and citizenship test: what is the difference?

The terms are easily confused:

- An **integration course** generally consists of a language course and an orientation course.
- The **orientation course** covers politics, history, law and society. It ends with the "Leben in Deutschland" (LiD) test.
- The **citizenship test** is the direct exam route for people who want to prove civic knowledge for their naturalisation.

Anyone already attending an integration course normally takes the LiD test. According to the [BAMF on naturalisation](https://www.bamf.de/DE/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html), a LiD result of at least 17 out of 33 points can also prove the knowledge required for naturalisation.

If you are not attending an integration course, you register for the citizenship test instead. You will find a detailed comparison in the article **[Leben in Deutschland test or citizenship test: the difference](/en/blog/unterschied-lid-test-einbuergerungstest/)**.

## Registering for the citizenship test without a course: step by step

### 1. First check with the naturalisation authority

Before registering, ask whether you actually need the citizenship test. A German school-leaving certificate or a suitable LiD certificate may be relevant depending on the case. The law also provides exceptions for illness, disability or age-related reasons.

This short check prevents you from booking an appointment unnecessarily. At the same time, the authority can name test centres near you.

### 2. Find an approved test centre

The test does not take place at home or as a freely bookable online exam. You need an approved test centre. These are often adult education centres, but other BAMF-approved providers are possible too.

Ask the test centre directly about:

- the next available date
- how to register
- the documents required
- how to pay
- the registration deadline
- cancelling or rebooking

Practical organisation can differ from centre to centre. So rely on the information from your chosen test centre. Our guide **[Registering for the citizenship test: step by step](/en/blog/anmeldung-pruefung-schritte/)** helps you plan your appointment.

### 3. Complete registration and pay the fee

Under [Section 2 of the Naturalisation Test Ordinance](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/einbtestv/BJNR164900008.html#BJNR164900008BJNE000200000), the exam date is arranged with a designated test centre. The ordinance sets a flat fee of 25 euros per participant.

The test centre will explain how to pay and whether you have to appear in person. The BAMF form asks, among other things, for your name, date of birth, place of birth and address. Make sure your details match your valid identity document.

### 4. Prepare the confirmation and exam day

Note the date, time and exact address. Have the identity document required by the test centre ready in good time. Also check when you should arrive and which writing materials are allowed or required.

Our **[exam day checklist](/en/blog/tipps-fuer-den-pruefungstag/)** offers a compact reminder for the appointment.

## How the exam works

The rules apply regardless of whether you attended a course. Under [Section 1 of the Naturalisation Test Ordinance](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/einbtestv/BJNR164900008.html#BJNR164900008BJNE000100000), a questionnaire contains 33 tasks. Each task has four answer options, one of which is correct.

The key numbers are:

- **33 exam questions** in total
- **30 general questions**
- **3 questions about your federal state**
- **60 minutes**
- **at least 17 correct answers** to pass

Your federal state depends on the place of residence used for the exam. So when preparing, check that you are not accidentally studying another state's questions. Our article on the **[federal state questions in the citizenship test](/en/blog/bundesland-fragen-erklaert/)** helps you deal with this part in a targeted way.

## Studying without a course: a practical four-step plan

Without lessons, what you mainly need is a clear order. Expensive study materials are not essential, because the BAMF provides the official set of questions.

### Step 1: Take a first test without preparation

Start with a simulation. The result shows you which topics you already know well and where the gaps are. Do not just write down the number of mistakes; assign each mistake to a topic: democracy, history, society or federal state.

### Step 2: Work through the official question catalogue completely

Use the [BAMF Online Test Centre](https://oet.bamf.de/ords/oetut/f?p=514:1::::::). For your personal preparation, the 300 general questions and the 10 questions for your federal state are what matter. Also read the wrong answer options. This helps you recognise similar terms more reliably later.

### Step 3: Keep a mistake list

Note every question you are unsure about with a short reason, for example "mixed up Bundestag and Bundesrat" or "missed the negation". Revise these questions first instead of starting the whole catalogue from the beginning again and again.

If you want a structured schedule, use the **[14-day plan for practising online](/en/blog/einbuergerungstest-online-ueben-2026/)**. It works completely without a course.

### Step 4: Simulate several complete exams

Answer 33 questions in one go and without help. Allow the same 60 minutes as in the real exam. The goal should not be to reach just 17 points once. It is safer to be clearly above the threshold in several simulations and to be able to explain your own mistakes.

## What the test does not replace

The citizenship test checks civic knowledge. It is not a language test and not complete proof of all naturalisation requirements. Any required B1 certificate, proof of identity, periods of residence, livelihood and other requirements are checked separately in the naturalisation procedure.

Particularly important: passing the test does not automatically mean that the citizenship application will be approved. It is only one building block of the procedure. Your nationality authority will tell you which documents and proof apply to you personally.

## Checklist: am I ready for the route without a course?

Check these points before registering:

- I have clarified whether I need the test certificate.
- I have found an approved test centre and a suitable date.
- I know how to pay, the registration deadline and the documents required.
- My personal details match my identity document.
- I practise with the official general questions and the questions for my federal state.
- I reliably score more than 17 points in several complete simulations.
- I know that the test does not replace any language certificate that may be required.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### Do I have to attend a preparation course before the citizenship test?

No. Section 10(5) StAG states explicitly that preparation courses are offered but attendance is not compulsory. You can study on your own and register directly with an approved test centre.

### Can I take the test before filing the citizenship application?

The current BAMF registration form explicitly offers the choice "citizenship application filed" or "not filed". Still, clarify with your authority when the test makes sense in your personal process.

### Do I need B1 to study for the test?

The citizenship test and the language certificate are different pieces of proof. The exam questions are written in German, so you must understand them well. Whether, and which, language certificate you need for naturalisation is checked separately by the authority.

### Is self-study worse than a course?

Not automatically. A course offers explanations, appointments and a fixed structure. With self-study you can choose your own pace and times. What matters is that you work with the official set of questions, revise mistakes in a targeted way and simulate complete tests.

### Will I get the result immediately after the test?

No. The binding certificate is issued after marking. Ask your test centre how the certificate is issued or sent in your case, and allow enough time for it.

## Next step

First clarify with your nationality authority whether you need the test. Then look for an approved test centre and start with the official question catalogue at the same time. That way you lose no study time even if you have to wait for an appointment.
