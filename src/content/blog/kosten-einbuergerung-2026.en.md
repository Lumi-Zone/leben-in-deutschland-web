If you want to become German, you need to invest some money as well as time and patience. Here is a detailed breakdown of the costs you may face in 2026.

## 1. The naturalisation fee
This is the biggest item. The fee for processing your application is set uniformly nationwide.
*   **Per person**: 255 euros
*   **For minor children** naturalised together with a parent: 51 euros

**Important**: if you can show that your income is low, the authority can reduce the fee in hardship cases or agree to payment in instalments. Just ask!

## 2. The citizenship test
If you do not have a German school-leaving certificate, you must pass the citizenship test (or the "Leben in Deutschland" test).
*   **Exam fee**: 25.00 euros
*   Registration is usually through the adult education centre (VHS).

## 3. Language certificates
You must prove German skills at level B1.
*   **Certificate exam (e.g. telc or Goethe)**: approx. 150–200 euros
*   If you attend an integration course, the final exam (DTZ) is often free or subsidised.

## 4. Documents and translations
You must submit many documents from your home country (birth certificate, marriage certificate, etc.).
*   **Certified translations**: approx. 50–300 euros depending on volume.
*   **Passport photos**: approx. 15 euros.

## 5. Your German ID
After you receive the naturalisation certificate, you will of course need German documents.
*   **ID card**: approx. 37 euros (from age 24). A fee increase has been announced, so check the current amount with your Bürgeramt.
*   **Passport**: approx. 70 euros (from age 24).

## Example of total costs
For a single person without children who has to do everything from scratch:
~ €255 (application) + €25 (test) + €200 (language) + €100 (translations) + €107 (ID documents)
**= approx. 687 euros**

So it pays to start saving early! But the investment in your future is definitely worth it.

## Quick summary
- Calculate fees and additional costs early.
- Besides official fees, translations and certificates can cause costs.
- A clear cost plan prevents delays in the process.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Which costs are often underestimated?
Certifications, translations and repeated applications are typical additional costs.

### Can I reduce costs?
Yes. Complete documents on the first attempt and early appointment planning help you avoid additional costs.

### When should I start planning my budget?
Ideally before your first appointment with the authority, so that fees and documents can be prepared without time pressure.
