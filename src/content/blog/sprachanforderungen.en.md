For naturalisation in Germany, sufficient knowledge of the German language is one of the most important requirements. But what exactly does "sufficient" mean?

## Level B1 of the CEFR
As a rule, you must prove German skills at level **B1** of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR).

At this level you can:
*   understand the main points of clear standard language on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, etc.
*   deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling in an area where the language is spoken.
*   produce simple connected text on topics that are familiar or of personal interest.
*   describe experiences and events, dreams, hopes and ambitions, and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.

## How do I prove my language skills?
There are several ways to prove level B1:

1.  **Zertifikat Deutsch / telc Deutsch B1**: an official language certificate from recognised exam providers (e.g. Goethe-Institut, telc).
2.  **German Test for Immigrants (DTZ)**: this test is often taken at the end of an integration course. If you reach level B1, it counts as proof.
3.  **School-leaving certificate**: a German school-leaving certificate (Hauptschulabschluss or higher) or studies at a German university is often also enough.
4.  **Vocational training**: completed vocational training in Germany.

## Exceptions
There are simplifications for certain groups, e.g. older people (the guest worker generation) or in the case of illness/disability. Ask your local naturalisation authority about this.

## Conclusion
Without B1 proof, naturalisation is usually not possible. Use integration courses or private language schools to reach this goal. The "Leben in Deutschland" test does **not** test your language skills but your knowledge about Germany – but of course you need to understand enough German to answer the questions!

## Quick summary
- For naturalisation you normally need German at B1 level.
- A language certificate, a DTZ result or a German qualification can serve as proof.
- The citizenship test does not replace a language certificate.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Is B1 enough for naturalisation?
As a rule, yes. But the authority always reviews the whole application, not just the language certificate.

### Does the "Leben in Deutschland" test count as proof of language skills?
No. The test shows knowledge about Germany. For German language skills you normally need separate proof.

### Are there exceptions to the B1 requirement?
Yes, exceptions are possible in certain cases, for example because of illness, disability, age or special rules. Ask your authority.
