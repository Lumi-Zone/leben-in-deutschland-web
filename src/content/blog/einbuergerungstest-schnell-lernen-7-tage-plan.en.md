Your appointment is soon and you are wondering: **Can I learn for the citizenship test quickly?** Yes, you can achieve a lot in a short time. But you need a clear plan. If you study without structure, you lose time and often revise the wrong things.

This 7-day plan is for people who want to study in a targeted way: with the official questions, the federal state questions and several exam simulations. It also works well if you have already attended an integration or orientation course and now want to revise.

## Before you start: what you really need to know

In the exam you get 33 questions. You have 60 minutes. For naturalisation you normally need at least 17 correct answers.

Three things matter for your preparation:

- the 300 general questions
- the 10 questions for your federal state
- complete tests with 33 questions

If you have little time, you cannot treat everything the same. Study the areas that cause the most mistakes first.

## Day 1: Overview and choosing your federal state

Do not start in a panic. Start with an overview.

Open the questions and answer the first blocks without pressure. The goal: you notice which topics you already know and which are difficult.

Do three things today:

1. Start with **[question 1](/en/frage/1/)**.
2. Select your federal state in the [question catalogue with all 16 federal states](/en/fragen/#bundeslaender).
3. Note difficult topics.

Typical difficult topics are:

- Bundestag, Bundesrat and federal government
- fundamental rights in the Basic Law
- elections and parties
- German history
- federal states and state politics

## Day 2: Politics and fundamental rights

Many questions are about democracy, law and fundamental rights. These terms also come up in the integration and orientation course.

Today, focus on:

- What is a democracy?
- Which rights does the Basic Law protect?
- What does freedom of expression mean?
- Who elects the Bundestag?
- What are the tasks of the federal government?

Read the questions slowly. If you do not understand a word, do not immediately look only for the correct answer. First try to understand the sentence.

## Day 3: History and society

On the third day it is about topics many learners mix up: history, the division of Germany, reunification, National Socialism and living together in society.

Do not just memorise dates. Remember simple connections:

- Why is the Basic Law important?
- Why does democracy play such a big role?
- What does responsibility mean in Germany?
- Which events are important in German history?

If you are unsure, also read **[The Basic Law explained simply](/en/blog/das-grundgesetz/)** and **[Elections in Germany](/en/blog/wahlen-in-deutschland/)**.

## Day 4: Federal state questions

Today is only about your federal state. Many people think: "It's only 10 questions, I'll do them later." That is exactly the mistake.

Open the right practice set, for example [Berlin](/en/berlin-fragen/), [Hamburg](/en/hamburg-fragen/), [Hesse](/en/hessen-fragen/), [Bavaria](/en/bayern-fragen/) or [North Rhine-Westphalia](/en/nordrhein-westfalen-fragen/). You will find all other states in the [question catalogue](/en/fragen/).

Learn:

- the state parliament (Landtag)
- the state government
- the Minister President
- the coat of arms
- the capital
- regional features

If you do not train your federal state reliably, you can easily lose points. So use the **[federal state questions](/en/#bundesland-fragen)** and select your own state.

## Day 5: Revise mistakes

Day five is the most important day. Now you no longer simply keep studying. Now you revise your mistakes.

For every wrong answer, ask yourself:

- Did I read the question too quickly?
- Did I confuse a word?
- Did I mix up two institutions?
- Was it a federal state question?
- Was it a history question?

This is where the app helps most: you can repeat difficult questions again and again and do short study sessions on the go. If you want to study on your phone, download the **Leben in Deutschland app** from the **[home page](/en/)**.

## Day 6: Exam simulation

Now it gets serious: take a complete simulation with 33 questions.

Important:

- no breaks
- no help
- calm concentration
- check your mistakes afterwards

Start here: **[citizenship test exam simulation](/en/exam/)**.

If you get fewer than 17 right, that is no reason to give up. Afterwards, revise only your mistakes. If you are just above 17, keep studying. It is safer to be clearly above 17.

## Day 7: Light revision, no overload

On the last day you should not learn everything again. Revise:

- wrong answers
- federal state questions
- key terms
- two or three difficult topics

Do at most one more simulation. After that, calm matters more than stress. Get your ID, appointment confirmation and travel plans ready.

## What you should avoid

### Only watching videos

Videos can help, but they do not replace actively answering the questions.

### Only taking sample tests

Sample tests show where you stand. But they do not replace revising the questions you got wrong.

### Forgetting your federal state

The state questions count. Learn them early, not on the evening before your appointment.

### Going to bed too late

Tiredness is a real source of mistakes. Many wrong answers come from careless reading.

## Why this plan helps learners make fast progress

The plan is short, but not chaotic. It combines three things that really help:

1. practising the question catalogue
2. recognising mistakes
3. simulating the real exam

That is exactly what this website and the **Leben in Deutschland app** are made for. You can start right away without spending ages looking for material. The questions, the test mode and the federal state topics are directly accessible.

## Quick summary

- You can achieve a lot in 7 days if you study in a structured way.
- Study the general questions first, then the federal state questions, then the simulation.
- Revise wrong answers in a targeted way.
- Take at least one complete exam simulation.
- Start right away with **[question 1](/en/frage/1/)** or practise on your phone with the app via the **[home page](/en/)**.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### Can you learn for the citizenship test in 7 days?

It depends on your prior knowledge. If you already know something about Germany, politics and history, a 7-day plan can help a lot. Without prior knowledge, you should allow more time.

### How many hours a day should I study?

Two or three short sessions are better than one very long session. For example, 30 minutes in the morning, 30 minutes in the afternoon and a review in the evening.

### What is more important: the 300 questions or the exam simulation?

Both are important. First you learn the questions. Then you use the simulation to check whether you would pass under real conditions.

### Do I really have to learn the federal state questions?

Yes. The exam also contains questions about your federal state. You should practise them in a targeted way.

### Does an app help to learn quickly?

Yes, especially for short revision sessions. An app makes it easier to study every day and not forget your mistakes.
