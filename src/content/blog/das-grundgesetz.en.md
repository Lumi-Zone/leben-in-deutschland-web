The **Basic Law** (Grundgesetz) is the constitution of the Federal Republic of Germany. It ranks above all other laws and governs how society and politics work together.

## Human dignity shall be inviolable
Article 1 is the most important sentence in the Basic Law. It means the state must protect and respect every person, no matter where they come from, what they believe or what they look like.

## The fundamental rights
Articles 1 to 19 guarantee the fundamental rights. They include:
*   **Freedom of expression**: everyone may state their opinion (as long as they do not insult anyone).
*   **Freedom of religion**: everyone may freely choose and practise their religion.
*   **Equality**: men and women have equal rights. No one may be disadvantaged because of their sex, origin, language or faith.

## The form of the state
The Basic Law establishes that Germany is a **democracy**, a **state governed by the rule of law** and a **social welfare state**.
*   **Democracy**: all state authority comes from the people (through elections).
*   **Rule of law**: the state, too, must obey the law. Courts are independent.
*   **Social welfare state**: the state looks after people who need help (for example through social assistance or pensions).

The Basic Law is the foundation of freedom in Germany. The "Leben in Deutschland" test asks many questions about it because it is so central to understanding German society.

## Quick summary
- The Basic Law is Germany's constitution.
- It protects fundamental rights such as human dignity, freedom and equality.
- Many questions in the citizenship test relate to the Basic Law.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### What is the Basic Law?
The Basic Law is the most important legal foundation in Germany. It sets out fundamental rights and the order of the state.

### Why is human dignity so important?
Human dignity stands at the very beginning of the Basic Law. It means every person has value and must be respected.

### Do I have to memorise article numbers?
Understanding usually matters more. You should know what the fundamental rights mean and why they are protected.
