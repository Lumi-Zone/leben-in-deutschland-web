Anyone who has to take the **German citizenship test** usually googles the same questions: How many questions are there? How many mistakes can I make? Can I take it online? What does the test cost? And what happens if I fail?

Here are short, clear answers. No panic, no bureaucratic jargon and no false promises.

## 1) What is the citizenship test?
The citizenship test (Einbürgerungstest) proves that you have basic knowledge about Germany. It covers the legal order, society, history, democracy and life in Germany.

In many cases this proof is important for naturalisation. Your naturalisation authority will tell you whether you need the test or whether an exception applies.

## 2) How many questions does the citizenship test have?
In the real exam you get **33 questions**. **30 are general questions** and **3 are about your federal state**.

This matters: you do not only learn about Germany in general, but also about the federal state where your main residence is registered.

More: **[How many questions are in the citizenship test?](/en/blog/wie-viele-fragen-hat-der-einbuergerungstest/)**

## 3) How many correct answers do I need?
For naturalisation you need at least **17 correct answers out of 33 questions**. This means you do not have to be perfect.

Even so, when practising you should not land just above 17. It is safer if you regularly score clearly higher in simulations.

More: **[How many correct answers do I need?](/en/blog/wie-viele-richtige-antworten-brauche-ich-einbuergerungstest/)**

## 4) How long does the citizenship test take?
You have **60 minutes**. For 33 questions this is usually very manageable.

Many candidates finish much earlier. Still, use the remaining time to calmly check your answers once more.

More: **[How long does the citizenship test take?](/en/blog/wie-lange-dauert-der-einbuergerungstest/)**

## 5) What do the questions look like?
They are multiple-choice questions. Each question has four answer options. One answer is correct.

The most common mistake is not a lack of knowledge but reading too quickly. Pay particular attention to words such as "not", "always", "only", "must" and "may".

## 6) How many questions can I study from?
The official question catalogue contains **310 questions**: 300 general questions and 10 questions about your federal state.

The exam is put together from this catalogue. That is why it makes sense not just to click through sample tests but to study the catalogue systematically.

## 7) Can I take the citizenship test online?
You can prepare online. The exam itself takes place at an approved test centre.

The BAMF provides an **[Online Test Centre for the citizenship test](https://www.bamf.de/DE/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/OnlineTestcenter/online-testcenter-node.html)**. There you can practise interactively and try sample papers.

More: **[Can I take the citizenship test online?](/en/blog/kann-ich-den-einbuergerungstest-online-machen/)**

## 8) What does the citizenship test cost?
According to the BAMF, taking the citizenship test costs **25 euros**.

Still, check the information from your test centre. There you will also find out how to pay and which documents you need to register.

More: **[What does the citizenship test cost?](/en/blog/was-kostet-der-einbuergerungstest/)**

## 9) What do I need to bring on exam day?
Bring a valid identity document with a photo. Without proof of identity, the test centre usually cannot assign your participation correctly.

Also useful: appointment confirmation, proof of payment, a pen, the address of the test centre and enough time to get there.

## 10) What happens if I fail?
If you answer fewer than 17 questions correctly, you can retake the test. That is annoying, but not a final failure.

What matters then is an honest analysis of your mistakes: were they federal state questions? Terms? Reading mistakes? Or was a real exam simulation missing?

## 11) Who does not have to take the test?
There are exceptions. The BAMF mentions, for example, cases where someone has a German school-leaving certificate or cannot meet the requirements because of illness, disability or age.

Clarify with the naturalisation authority whether an exception applies in your case. Do not rely only on short internet answers when it comes to exceptions.

## 12) How do I best get started?
Start with three steps:

- select your federal state
- split the 300 general questions into small blocks
- consistently revise the answers you got wrong

If you only want to get one thing right: do not study chaotically. A list of your mistakes is worth more than hours of clicking through questions.

## Quick summary
- The citizenship test has 33 questions, 60 minutes and four answer options per question.
- For naturalisation you need at least 17 correct answers.
- The exam consists of 30 general questions and 3 questions about your federal state.
- There are 310 questions to prepare with: 300 general questions and 10 federal state questions.
- The exam takes place at a test centre; online you can mainly practise.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### How many mistakes can I make in the citizenship test?
If 17 of 33 answers must be correct, up to 16 answers can be wrong. As a study goal, however, that is very tight.

### Is the citizenship test difficult?
For most people it is very manageable if they practise with the original questions. It mainly becomes difficult without the federal state questions and without revising mistakes.

### Are there oral questions?
The citizenship test is a written multiple-choice test. For each question you choose one of four answers.

### Can I study with sample tests only?
Sample tests help, but they are often not enough. A combination of question catalogue, federal state questions, a mistake list and exam simulation is better.

### When will I get my result?
The result does not come immediately like an online quiz. You will later receive a certificate with your personal test result.
