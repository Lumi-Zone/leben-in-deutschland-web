He had everything the law requires: German at B1 level, a passed Leben in Deutschland test, a full-time job and more than ten years of residence. Even so, **Peine district** (Landkreis Peine) refused his naturalisation. The reason was his answers to **23 questions** he was asked at his appointment, including "Do you watch German television?" and "Do you regularly visit a mosque?"

On 20 February 2025, the **Administrative Court of Braunschweig** (Verwaltungsgericht Braunschweig) overturned the refusal (case no. 4 A 114/24). Its core message: without concrete indications that cast doubt on an applicant's loyalty to the constitution, there is **no legal basis for questioning without a reason**. For everyone who wants to be naturalised, this is one of the most important rulings since the 2024 reform.

> **Note:** This article provides general information, not legal advice. The ruling is a first-instance decision and binds only the parties to the case. If you face a refusal, get advice from a migration advice service or a lawyer specialising in migration law.

## Key points

- A Lebanese man, living in Germany since 2013, applied for naturalisation in November 2023 and submitted all the required evidence.
- At his appointment, he had to answer 23 questions, among other things about democracy, basic rights, the news, the national anthem, mosque visits and his TV viewing. His answers were recorded.
- In April 2024, Peine district refused: he could not make an effective commitment (Bekenntnis) because he lacked basic knowledge.
- The Administrative Court of Braunschweig overturned the decision. The office has to decide again.
- Core statements: no examination of attitudes or knowledge without a reason; the passed naturalisation test proves the required knowledge; doubts require **factual indications** (tatsächliche Anhaltspunkte).
- The court allowed an appeal and a leapfrog appeal (Sprungrevision) to the Federal Administrative Court. We found no decision by the highest court on this at the time of writing.

## What exactly happened?

The claimant was born in Beirut in 1978 and has lived in Germany since 2013. He submitted his application on 28 November 2023. He provided a B1 certificate, the passed Leben in Deutschland test, proof of his full-time employment and the other documents.

At the appointment, he was told that his commitment to the free democratic basic order had to reflect his genuine conviction. For this reason, the office would check whether he had basic knowledge of this order. Then came a questioning with 23 questions. According to the court's findings, he was not given a commitment form or a declaration of loyalty (Loyalitätserklärung) at that point.

On 3 April 2024, the district refused the application. It said he had not answered the bulk of the questions fully and correctly and lacked even "rudimentary basic knowledge". He was therefore unable to make an effective commitment.

## The questions he was asked

According to the judgment, the 23 questions covered very different areas:

| Area | Examples from the case |
| --- | --- |
| Personal life and values | Why do you want to be naturalised? Which values do you associate with Germany? Do you watch German television? Do you regularly visit a mosque? |
| Constitution and state | What do you understand by democracy? What is the rule of law, what does the independence of the courts mean? What does Article 1 of the Basic Law say? Name three basic rights. Questions on press freedom, the form of state and the principles of elections |
| General knowledge | The beginning of the national anthem, the number of federal states, current news, wars, events in Israel |
| Religion | His view of the tradition about the Prophet Muhammad's marriage to Aisha |

The court noted that many of these questions have no objectively verifiable correct answer or no clear link to the free democratic basic order. Some of the claimant's answers, such as "Federal Republic" as the form of state or "16" as the number of federal states, had not been wrong and had not been taken into account by the office.

## What the court decided

**1. The commitment is a real requirement.** Since the Nationality Modernisation Act of 2024 (Staatsangehörigkeitsmodernisierungsgesetz), it is clear that the commitment must be based on genuine conviction. Mere lip service is not enough.

**2. Doubts require facts.** Under [Section 11 sentence 1 no. 1a StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__11.html), naturalisation is excluded if **factual indications** suggest that the commitment is untrue. The office must look into such indications. But it may not simply assume them.

**3. No legal basis for questioning without a reason.** Without indications of statements or conduct that give rise to doubts about loyalty to the constitution, the Nationality Act provides no basis for questioning all applicants. The court referred to the legislative explanatory memorandum and the federal application guidelines, according to which a general check of applicants' inner attitude is specifically not carried out.

**4. The naturalisation test counts.** Knowledge of the legal and social order is proven by the passed naturalisation test. The office may not introduce its own freely designed knowledge test on top of it.

The court ordered the district to decide again in line with this legal view. Because of the fundamental importance of the case, it allowed an appeal and a leapfrog appeal to the Federal Administrative Court.

## What has changed since then?

In a response to the state parliament in May 2025 ([printed paper 19/7141](https://www.landtag-niedersachsen.de/drucksachen/drucksachen_19_07500/07001-07500/19-07141.pdf)), the Lower Saxony authorities described their practice:

- **Peine district** stated that there was no general check of applicants' inner attitude. Only where there are concrete indications is there a personal, open-ended conversation with its own question catalogue.
- The **City of Hameln** reported that, most recently, questioning had taken place in every case; the procedure is now being changed so that questions are only asked where there are indications.
- The Lower Saxony Ministry of the Interior views routine "knowledge tests" without a reason critically.

Other offices, by contrast, continue to ask questions regularly, for example the City of Hannover with a written question catalogue. For an overview, see our article **[Naturalisation in Lower Saxony: how offices check the commitment](/en/blog/einbuergerung-niedersachsen-bekenntnis-befragung/)**.

## What the ruling means for you

**If you are questioned:** The ruling does not give you a right to simply refuse a questionnaire. A refusal can trigger doubts and delay your case. Answer the questions calmly, honestly and in your own words. Our guide **[German citizenship interview questions](/en/blog/einbuergerung-gespraech-fragen-bekenntnis/)** shows what good answers look like.

**If your application is about to be refused because of your answers:**

1. **Ask for a copy** of the questionnaire or the record. Check whether your answers have been reproduced correctly.
2. **Request access to your file** (Akteneinsicht) to see what the office bases its doubts on. Are there factual indications, or were you questioned without a reason?
3. **Respond if you are given a hearing** (Anhörung). Point to your passed naturalisation test and your signed commitment.
4. **Watch the deadline.** Which legal remedy applies and how long the deadline is can be found in the legal remedy information (Rechtsbehelfsbelehrung) at the end of the decision.
5. **Get support**, for example from a migration advice service or a lawyer specialising in migration law. The Braunschweig ruling is an important argument here.

**Important:** The ruling does not protect you from a refusal if there are genuine indications, for example antisemitic statements, support for extremist groups or conduct that disregards equality between men and women. Our article **[When naturalisation is excluded](/en/blog/einbuergerung-ausschlussgruende-antisemitismus-mehrehe/)** explains which grounds rule out naturalisation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the ruling apply throughout Germany?

No. A ruling by an administrative court directly binds only the parties. Other courts and offices may decide differently. However, its reasoning is based on the legislative explanatory memorandum and the federal application guidelines. Only the Federal Administrative Court can settle the question for good.

### Is the office no longer allowed to ask any questions?

It is. It may explain the content of the commitment to you and ask whether you have understood it. If there are factual indications for doubts, it may also ask more detailed questions. According to the ruling, what is not permitted is a general examination of knowledge or attitudes without a reason.

### Do I need to know how the national anthem begins?

The law requires knowledge that you prove with the naturalisation test. Even so, general knowledge about Germany is worthwhile, because some offices ask about it. Practise the **[naturalisation test questions](/en/fragen/)**, especially on basic rights, democracy and history.

### What does the case mean for people who are religious?

Religious practice such as visiting a mosque is protected by religious freedom and is not in itself a reason for doubt. The court expressly viewed questions without an objectively verifiable answer critically. More in the article **[Religionsfreiheit in Deutschland](/en/blog/religionsfreiheit/)** (in German).
