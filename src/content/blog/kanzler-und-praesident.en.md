Germany has a head of state and a head of government. But who is who?

## The Federal President (head of state)
The Federal President (Bundespräsident) is Germany's **head of state**.
*   **Tasks**: represents Germany abroad (under international law), signs laws so that they become valid and proposes the Federal Chancellor for election.
*   **Power**: has little direct political power (a largely representative role) but is an important moral authority.
*   **Election**: elected by the **Federal Convention** (Bundesversammlung), not directly by the people, for 5 years.

## The Federal Chancellor (head of government)
The Federal Chancellor (Bundeskanzler) is the **head of government**.
*   **Tasks**: sets the "guidelines of policy" (Richtlinienkompetenz), chooses the federal ministers and runs the business of government.
*   **Power**: holds the power to shape politics.
*   **Election**: elected by the **Bundestag**.

Remember: the Chancellor governs, the President represents.

## Quick summary
- The Federal Chancellor leads the federal government.
- The Federal President represents Germany abroad and has mainly representative tasks.
- The citizenship test often checks the difference indirectly.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)
### Who leads the federal government?
The Federal Chancellor leads the federal government and sets the main lines of policy.

### Is the Federal President the head of government?
No. The Federal President is the head of state. The head of government is the Federal Chancellor.

### Why do many people confuse the two offices?
Both offices are very important. But they have different tasks: leading the government and representing the state.
