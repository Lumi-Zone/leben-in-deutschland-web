**Since 27 June 2024, Germany generally allows dual citizenship. There is no separate "application for dual citizenship".** If you naturalise, you file the normal naturalisation application and no longer have to give up your previous citizenship. If you are German and acquire another citizenship on application, you no longer lose your German citizenship as a result.

Whether you can actually keep both passports often depends on the **other country**. This guide is for orientation only and is not legal advice.

## Case 1: You are naturalising in Germany

In the past, many applicants had to give up their previous citizenship. This requirement was abolished with the reform of German nationality law: in [Section 10(1) StAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html) the former number 4 is marked as "repealed".

For you this means:

- You file the **normal naturalisation application** with your naturalisation authority: [application and documents](/en/blog/einbuergerung-antrag-checkliste-2026/).
- You meet the usual [requirements for naturalisation](/en/blog/german-citizenship-requirements/), including B1 German and the citizenship test.
- Germany does not require you to be released from your previous citizenship.

## Case 2: You are German and want a second citizenship

Until June 2024, Germans usually lost their citizenship automatically when they acquired a foreign one on application, unless they had first obtained a **retention permit** (Beibehaltungsgenehmigung). The rule on this, Section 25 StAG, has been [repealed](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__25.html).

According to the [Federal Foreign Office](https://www.germany.info/us-de/service/staatsangehoerigkeit/beibehaltung-der-deutschen-staatsangehoerigkeit-1216762), the following has therefore applied since 27 June 2024:

- An **application to retain German citizenship is no longer necessary**, for example when naturalising in the USA or another country.
- You do not have to inform German authorities either.
- The new rule does **not apply retroactively**. Anyone who lost German citizenship before 27 June 2024 does not automatically get it back.

## What does your country of origin say?

Germany accepts multiple citizenship, but not every country does:

- Some countries allow dual citizenship without restriction.
- Others require a permit or a declaration before you acquire another citizenship.
- Some countries automatically withdraw their own citizenship if you acquire another one. India, for example, does not allow dual citizenship.

Clarify this **before** you apply, with the embassy or consulate of your country of origin. Also ask about possible consequences for inheritance, property, military service or pensions in your country of origin.

## Benefits of German citizenship

With a German passport you have, among other things, the right to vote in federal and state elections, full freedom of movement in the EU and consular protection abroad. Your previous citizenship can additionally keep your link to your country of origin.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to hand in my old passport when I naturalise?

Under German law, no. Whether you may keep it is decided by your country of origin.

### Can I get my old citizenship back later?

If you were naturalised before 27 June 2024 and had to give up your old citizenship, you can only reacquire it under the law of your country of origin. Ask there. From the German point of view, such an acquisition no longer leads to the loss of German citizenship today.

### Which office is responsible?

For naturalisation in Germany, the naturalisation authority where you live; for the law of your country of origin, its embassy or consulate; and for Germans abroad, the German mission abroad.
